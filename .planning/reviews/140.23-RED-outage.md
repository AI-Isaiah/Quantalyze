## Headline

Three new answers to the question that matters most:

1. **An MT5 gateway outage still takes key-connect down for every venue** — the exact A-01
   harm the 140.2-06 per-dependency split was written to remove, half-removed. (O1, HIGH)
2. **An Upstash outage tells 11 of the 12 rate-limited seam routes to refuse every request,
   and the wizard renders that as "The exchange rate-limited this request… wait 60 seconds"
   on the user's *first* click** — while the breaker sharing that same store silently fails
   open. Same store, two opposite postures, one fabricated sentence about a third party.
   (O2, HIGH)
3. **`SyncPreviewStep` — the wizard's sync surface — recognises none of the seam's four wire
   codes.** A breaker trip renders "Sync failed… we cannot tell which step failed", and the
   primary control beside it is "Try another key", which deletes the draft and every

## O1 — HIGH — `breaker:mt5-gateway` opening denies key-connect on **every** venue

**Files.** `src/lib/resilient-fetch.ts:392-402` (the `validate-key` budget row),
`:804-806` (`breakerKeysFor`), `:990-1024` (`isBreakerOpen`);
`analytics-service/routers/exchange.py:331-350` (the two 503 sites).

**Trace (all steps MEASURED at source).**

1. `exchange.py:331-350` — `_validate_mt5_key` raises `service_error(503,
   "MT5_GATEWAY_UNREACHABLE", dependency="mt5-gateway", retry_after=30)` on a gateway
   connect timeout **and** on any connect exception. The in-file comment says the modal cause
   is *"a gateway restart on every deploy"*.
2. `seamBreakerVerdict(503, body)` → `serviceDependencyOf` reads `body.detail.dependency`,
   finds `mt5-gateway` in the frozen set → `counts:true`, `breakerKey:"breaker:mt5-gateway"`
   (`seam-discriminator.ts:171-190`, `:445-457`). 5 inside 30 s arms the lock.
3. `SEAM_BUDGETS["validate-key"].dependencies = ["mt5-gateway"]` (`resilient-fetch.ts:396`).
4. `breakerKeysFor("validate-key")` = `["breaker:mt5-gateway", "breaker:railway"]`
   (`:804-806`). `isBreakerOpen` returns on the **first live lock** (`:1005-1017`).
5. Therefore *every* call spending the `validate-key` budget short-circuits — and that budget
   is spent by `validateKey()`, which is the **first** upstream call of
   `strategies/create-with-key` (`:286`), `keys/validate-and-encrypt`
   (`legacyValidateAndEncryptHandler`), and `strategies/composite/add-key`.
6. Binance / Deribit / OKX / Bybit / sFOX users connecting a key get
   `CircuitOpenError` → `classifyKeyValidationError` → `SERVICE_UNAVAILABLE_RETRY` 503 →
   ConnectKeyStep renders *"Our service is temporarily unavailable… this request was never
   sent."* Which is true — and is also a total key-connect outage caused by a dependency
   those users can never reach.

**Why this is a defect and not the design.** `resilient-fetch.ts:87-95` states the split's
purpose as removing *"obligation OB-8's harm (A-01: one MT5 gateway restart denying every
Deribit user, the optimizer and CSV finalize)"*. The optimizer (`dependencies: []`, `:426`)
and CSV finalize (`process-key-sync`, `:473`) **are** now protected. The *first-named* victim
— every Deribit user's key connect — is not, because containment is keyed on the BUDGET ROW
and the budget row is per-ENDPOINT: `/api/validate-key` serves all venues and only its MT5
arm can raise the 503. The docblock's own tie-break ("when in doubt, declare fewer") points
at this row and was not applied to it.

**Reachability.** The Python limiter on `/api/validate-key` is `100/hour` per tenant
(`exchange.py:467-468`), and a gateway that is *down* (connection refused) returns
immediately rather than after `_MT5_PROBE_TIMEOUT_S`, so one MT5 user retrying, or two or
three concurrently, produces 5 failures well inside the 30 s window. MEASURED for the code
path; the arrival rate is INFERRED.

**Duty cycle, not a blip.** While the lock is live MT5 requests are short-circuited too, so
they cannot re-trip it; after 30 s it closes, MT5 traffic returns, and 5 more failures re-arm
it. With sustained MT5 retry traffic, non-MT5 key-connect is denied most of the time. The
`[30 s, 60 s]` recovery band at `:150-183` applies on top.

**Cheapest fix that keeps OB-8:** condition the declared dependency on the request, not the
route — or split `validate-key` into `validate-key` / `validate-key-mt5` budget rows, which
costs one union member and no cross-language change.

**Not covered by any existing register.** `RED-attacker.md:152` covers the *global* key DoS;
`SPEC-silent-failure.md` C1 covers two MT5 arms that became breaker-**inert** (the opposite
direction).

## O2 — HIGH — an Upstash outage refuses every seam request and the wizard blames the user's exchange

**Files.** `src/lib/ratelimit.ts:303-337` (`checkLimit`), `:244-260`
(`isRateLimitMisconfigured`), `:291` (`MISCONFIGURED_RETRY_AFTER_S = 60`);
`src/lib/resilient-fetch.ts:1025-1032`, `:1236-1243` (the two breaker store catches);
`src/app/api/strategies/create-with-key/route.ts:223-235`;
`src/lib/wizardErrors.ts:464-475` (`KEY_RATE_LIMIT` copy).

**Trace (MEASURED).** One store, two opposite postures on the same failure:

| component | Upstash rejects | result |
|---|---|---|
| `checkLimit` (`ratelimit.ts:322-336`) | `catch` → `isProduction()` → `{success:false, retryAfter:60, reason:"ratelimit_misconfigured"}` | **fails CLOSED** |
| `isBreakerOpen` (`resilient-fetch.ts:1025-1031`) | `catch` → `console.error` → `{open:false}` | **fails OPEN** |

So during an Upstash outage the breaker is entirely disabled (the known C2) **and** every
limiter denies. The limiter runs first in every seam route, so the user never reaches the
seam at all — they get a 429.

**What renders.** `checkLimit` hands back `reason:"ratelimit_misconfigured"` precisely so the
caller can answer 503 instead of 429. I audited all 15 seam routes with
`grep -c 'checkLimit(' / 'isRateLimitMisconfigured'`:

- **Split present (3):** `strategies/finalize-wizard:590`, `bridge:88`, `simulator:113`.
- **No limiter (1):** `admin/match/eval`.
- **Split ABSENT (11):** `keys/validate-and-encrypt`, `strategies/create-with-key`,
  `strategies/composite/add-key`, `keys/sync` (×2 limiters), `keys/[id]/permissions`,
  `portfolio-optimizer`, `scenario/optimize`, `admin/match/recompute`, `verify-strategy`,
  `strategies/csv-validate`, `strategies/csv-finalize`.

The 11 all answer `429 "Too many requests"`. The worst render is `create-with-key:228`, which
attaches `code: "KEY_RATE_LIMIT"`, which `ConnectKeyStep` admits
(`KNOWN_CREATE_WITH_KEY_CODES`, `:222`) and renders as:

> **"The exchange rate-limited this request."**
> *"The exchange asked us to slow down. This is a transient, exchange-side throttle and not a
> problem with your key."* — fix: *"Wait 60 seconds and try again."*

Every clause is false. No exchange was contacted, our own store is down, the user is on their
first click, and 60 seconds does not help. `SyncPreviewStep` renders `RATE_LIMITED` — *"We cap
how often this action can run and this attempt went over the cap"* — also false on a first
request. `PortfolioOptimizer` and `KeyPermissionBadge` render a bare "Too many requests" to an
allocator's first click.

**Relationship to the known finding.** The register records `KEY_RATE_LIMIT` blaming the venue
for our own limiter (`SPEC-silent-failure.md:715`). What is new is the **trigger**: the sentence
is not reserved for users who actually exceeded a cap. A store outage fires it for *every user
on every seam action simultaneously*, which is also exactly when a false "this is the
exchange's fault" is most expensive. And the discriminator to avoid it already exists, is
already used at 22 non-seam routes, and was not applied to 11 of the 15 routes this phase
spent 140 commits hardening.

**For how long:** until Upstash recovers. Nothing self-heals, nothing pages — the limiter arm
logs `console.error("[ratelimit] check failed:")` and the breaker arm logs
`console.error("breaker check failed — failing OPEN")`; `grep captureToSentry src/lib/resilient-fetch.ts src/lib/ratelimit.ts` → **0**.

**Secondary (latency, MEASURED):** the breaker read is `Redis.fromEnv({retries: 1, backoff:
250 ms, signal: AbortSignal.timeout(2000)})` (`:716-725`, `:285-306`), so an unresponsive
store adds up to ~4.25 s to *every* seam call before the request is even issued — on top of
the up-to-5 s `@upstash/ratelimit` timeout sentinel inside `recordSeamFailure` (`:1088-1099`).

## O3 — HIGH — the wizard's sync surface recognises **none** of the seam's wire codes, and offers a destructive control

**Files.** `src/app/(dashboard)/strategies/new/wizard/steps/SyncPreviewStep.tsx:147-174`
(`KNOWN_KICKOFF_CODES`), `:593-631` (the read), `:1338-1355`
(`DESTRUCTIVE_CONTROL_IS_WRONG_FOR`), `:1379-1400` (the render);
`src/lib/process-key-client.ts:439-462` (`CIRCUIT_OPEN`), `:509-529` (`UPSTREAM_TIMEOUT`),
`:531-556` (`UPSTREAM_NETWORK_ERROR`), `:481-507` (`SEAM_MISCONFIGURED`);
`src/app/api/keys/sync/route.ts:544` (`if (!result.ok) return result.response;`);
`src/app/(dashboard)/strategies/new/wizard/WizardClient.tsx:864-880`, `:615-660`.

**Trace (MEASURED end to end).**

1. Breaker open. `postProcessKey` returns `503 {ok:false, code:"CIRCUIT_OPEN",
   human_message: <the seam-copy sentence>, correlation_id, recoverable:true}` +
   `Retry-After` (`process-key-client.ts:445-461`).
2. `keys/sync/route.ts:544` forwards it **verbatim**, status and body.
3. `SyncPreviewStep:601-604`: `KNOWN_KICKOFF_CODES[failure.code] || "SYNC_FAILED"`. The table
   holds exactly `RATE_LIMITED`, `GATE_DRAFT_GONE`, `COMPOSITE_MEMBERSHIP_UNKNOWN`,
   `MISSING_STRATEGY_ID`, `INVALID_STRATEGY_ID`. `CIRCUIT_OPEN` is absent →
   **`SYNC_FAILED`**. Same for `UPSTREAM_TIMEOUT` (Railway hung), `UPSTREAM_NETWORK_ERROR`
   (transport), and `SEAM_MISCONFIGURED` (our own config typo, added in this range at
   `dc6a4030`).
4. Rendered copy (`wizardErrors.ts:531-542`): *"Sync failed. The sync did not complete. **We
   cannot tell from here which step failed or how far it got.**"* — while the wire told us
   precisely: no request was ever sent, nothing was submitted, retry in N seconds.
5. `SYNC_FAILED` is recoverable, so `ErrorEnvelope` renders Retry — and the step's own
   primary button renders too, because `SYNC_FAILED ∉ DESTRUCTIVE_CONTROL_IS_WRONG_FOR`
   (`:1348-1355`, which holds only `GATE_DRAFT_GONE` and `VALIDATION_FAILED`). That button is
   **"Try another key"** → `WizardClient:876` `void handleDeleteDraft()` → an unconfirmed
   `DELETE /api/strategies/draft/{id}`, which cascades every `strategy_keys` member.

**Why this is the phase's own defect and not pre-existing.** `SubmitStep`, the sibling step,
does the translation: `recogniseSeamErrorCode(data.code)` **before** the membership check
(`SubmitStep.tsx:207-212`), added by 140.3-05 for exactly this reason, with a docblock
(`:196-206`) that says *"the wire vocabulary and the wizard vocabulary are not the same set…
all three failed the membership check below and fell to UNKNOWN."* 140.3-10 then built
`KNOWN_KICKOFF_CODES` at `SyncPreviewStep` as a raw wire-string lookup with no translation
hop, five plans later, on the route that forwards the seam envelope most directly. The class
was named, fixed once, and re-created at the second site in the same phase.

**Why the destructive control matters here specifically.** `WizardClient:699-727` is a
140.3-10 comment arguing at length that `start_fresh` must go through a confirmation because
*"one click on a control offered by an ERROR STATE deleted the draft and every strategy_keys
member under it, with no way back"*. `onTryAnotherKey` was left unconfirmed **deliberately**,
on the stated grounds that it is *"for a REJECTED key"*. That premise holds for
`KEY_AUTH_FAILED`. It does not hold for a breaker trip, a Railway hang, or our own
misconfiguration — three states in which the key is provably not the problem and the copy on
screen says so.

**Fix:** route `failure.code` through `recogniseSeamErrorCode` first (one line, the identical
shape as `SubmitStep:207-212`), and add the resulting codes plus `SYNC_FAILED` to
`DESTRUCTIVE_CONTROL_IS_WRONG_FOR` — the flag's own docblock says it is *"about the PROPERTY…
not about one code"*.

**Adjacent, not duplicate:** `SPEC-silent-failure.md` C0 flags the same button for the *gate*
codes reached via a discarded Supabase read; the register's secondary LOW at `:515-522` flags
`KNOWN_KICKOFF_CODES`' prototype-`Record` shape. Neither notices that the seam's own four
codes are missing from the table.

## O4 — MEDIUM-HIGH — the publish gate flattens every probe failure into one sentence that asserts two things it cannot know

**Files.** `src/app/api/strategies/finalize-wizard/route.ts:167-192` (`fetchLivePermissions`),

**Trace (MEASURED).** `fetchLivePermissions:190-192` does
`if (!res.ok) throw new Error(\`permissions probe failed: ${res.status}\`)` — the status is
stringified into a message and every other fact on the wire (the machine `code`, the

`{error:"Could not verify key scopes", code:"KEY_NETWORK_TIMEOUT"}` for all of them.
`SubmitStep` admits `KEY_NETWORK_TIMEOUT` and renders:

> **"We could not reach the exchange."**
> *"The validation request did not complete in time. Usually means a temporary exchange issue
> or a network blip on our side."*

Producers this collapses (read at `analytics-service/routers/internal.py`):

| wire | what actually happened | rendered as |
|---|---|---|
| `429 RATE_LIMITED` (per-key probe throttle, PYAPIFIX2-03) | **our own service** throttled us, and said for how long | "could not reach the exchange … did not complete in time" |
| `500 KEK_UNAVAILABLE` (`:300`, `:316`) | **our** encryption key is unset; needs an operator | same |
| `424 EXCHANGE_PROBE_FAILED` (`:495`) | the venue genuinely failed | same (roughly true) |

Two of the three assert a completed-response-that-never-came about a service that *did*
answer, and one blames the user's exchange for our unset KEK — the theme-2 lie SEAMUX-04

**Why it is this phase's miss.** Commit `4986584b` (140.3-09 / TS-34) fixed exactly this on the

what `finalize-wizard` still does, at the publish gate, on the same probe. The mechanism
(`buildSeamFailureCause` / `readSeamFailureCause`) is already written and is route-local.

Failing CLOSED is correct and must not change; only the envelope is wrong.

## Scenario answers, stated plainly

**1. Railway hung (deadline hit).**
- *Wizard key-connect*: `AnalyticsTimeoutError` → `classifyKeyValidationError` matches
  `"timeout"` → `KEY_NETWORK_TIMEOUT` 502 → *"We could not reach the exchange."* **False**
  — the request never left our stack. Already registered (`SPEC-silent-failure.md` H8).
- *CSV finalize*: `UPSTREAM_TIMEOUT` 504 → `CsvSubmitStep:187-197` takes `data.code` and
  `data.human_message` verbatim → *"The ingestion service did not respond in time."* **True.**
- *Allocator optimizer*: 504 `{status:"failed", error:"Optimizer timed out"}` →
  `PortfolioOptimizer:219-230` → renders it. **True.**
- *Admin match recompute*: 504 `TIMEOUT_COPY` → `AllocatorMatchQueue:274-278`. **True.**
- *Wizard sync kickoff*: `UPSTREAM_TIMEOUT` → **`SYNC_FAILED`** (O3).

**2. Railway 500 bodyless `text/plain`.** `seamBreakerVerdict` correctly refuses to count it
(`seam-discriminator.ts:437-444`) — that half is right and is load-bearing (it closes the
cheapest self-inflicted-outage variant). Downstream: `postProcessKey` cannot parse it, so
`body = {}` and `:637-641` forwards **`500 {}`** — no code, no `human_message`, no
`correlation_id`. `SubmitStep` → `UNKNOWN`; `SyncPreviewStep` → `SYNC_FAILED`; both are
honest-but-blind, and both are strictly worse than what the status line already told us.
Via `analyticsRequest` the body becomes `AnalyticsUpstreamError("Internal Server Error", 500)`
→ `UNKNOWN` (MEASURED-BY-REPLAY). No surface lies.

**3. Upstash down.** See O2. Compound answer: every seam route denies (11 of 12 as a 429 that
blames a rate limit that was never hit), the breaker is silently disabled, and the only signal
is two `console.error` lines with zero Sentry captures. Duration: unbounded.

**4. Breaker OPEN across the 15 routes.** 13 of 15 surfaces render the breaker's own sentence
or a truthful equivalent. Two do not: `SyncPreviewStep` (O3, the serious one) and
`WeightOptimizerSection` (O6, benign).

**5. Venue down vs caller's key wrong.** The two known 424-attribution defects (C1/H2/H3/L1)
stand. New adjacent instance: `PERMISSION_DENIED` renders as an IP-allowlist problem (O5).

**6. Partial/degraded result rendering as complete.** M2 (`portfolio-optimizer` re-stamping
`status:"complete"`) confirmed at `route.ts:158-166`; nothing new to add.

## Lanes walked and found CLEAN — do not re-audit

- **`unstable_cache` × breaker at `keys/[id]/permissions`.** The claim at `:213-224` holds:
  `CircuitOpenError` and `SeamBodyReadError` both **throw out of** the cached callback
  (`:218`, `:330`), the fork writes no entry for a rejected callback, so a 503 cannot outlive
  the 30 s cooldown inside the 60 s cache. Traced, correct.
- **`readDependencyBody`'s tee** (`resilient-fetch.ts:1567-1580`). `res.clone().json()` on a
  503 only; the caller's branch is untouched; every failure mode returns `undefined` and the
  verdict still counts against the global key. Cannot make the key *less* specific or change
  whether recording happens. Correct.
- **The `recordOnce` latch** (`:1727-1737`). A counting status plus an aborting body records
  once. Verified against both call sites (`:1837`, `:1504`).
- **`instrumentBody`'s `SyntaxError` passthrough** (`:1464-1472`). A parse failure is
  rethrown raw and the breaker hears nothing — correct, and it is what keeps a `text/plain`
  503 from double-counting.
- **`SeamConfigError` above the classification window** (`:1642-1692`). An invalid
  `timeoutMsOverride` or malformed base URL is logged as a config fault, records nothing, and
