## Summary

Every headline number in CONTEXT.md was independently re-derived. **Most reproduce; three do not**, and
one CONTEXT scope item **has already landed** and should be struck from the phase. The re-derivations
also surfaced four defects larger than CONTEXT describes them, and one entirely new one on the CSV
validation arm.

The single highest-leverage finding is that **DEF-140.4-C (§4a) and the §6 hand-off-hole residual are
the same defect**. `CsvUploadStep.tsx` and `CsvSubmitStep.tsx` are the only two wizard clients that
never call `seamErrorCode` / `recogniseSeamErrorCode`; both consume `rateLimitDenyJson` routes; and
that missing hop is *mechanically why* a forwarded 401/403 renders as `CSV_VALIDATION_FAILED`. One
artefact closes both scope items. That is coverage-law **row 1**.

The second is that **the citation guard §4b asks for is vacuous under the obvious predicate.** On the
34-file seam surface there are 51 citations and **zero** are past-EOF — while the citations that *are*
provably wrong there (`exchange.py:314,324`, `route.ts:748`, `permissions/route.ts:166`'s "5 minutes")
are all *in range*. A past-EOF guard scoped to the seam would be green on day one and forever. The
guard has to check something else, and §10 measures the three viable options.

**Primary recommendation:** land two row-1 artefacts — (1) route the two CSV wizard clients through the
shared wire→wizard hop and add the property guard that every `rateLimitDenyJson`-consuming client does
so; (2) forbid bare `file:line` citations on the seam surface and convert the 51 that exist. Then do
the enumerated per-site work (`Retry-After` threads, `SERVICE_UNREACHABLE`, the venue table, test
fidelity) with the counts in §4–§9, which differ from CONTEXT's in five places.

## 17. Common pitfalls specific to this phase

### Pitfall 1: shipping a guard that is green because it measures nothing
**What goes wrong:** a past-EOF citation guard scoped to the seam surface passes forever.
**Why:** §3.7 — 0 of 51 seam citations are past-EOF, while 9 are provably wrong.
**Avoid:** every absence-assertion needs a **vacuity fence** (a lower bound on what it scanned) and a
**positive control** (a synthetic bad input it does catch). `seam-log-coverage.test.ts:962-1013` is the
in-repo template and already does both.
**Warning sign:** the guard's failure message cannot name a concrete thing it would catch.

### Pitfall 2: a grep that matches the file's own prose (DEF-16-2)
**What goes wrong:** §8.3 — widening `ci.yml:1633` to `src/**` reddens a clean tree, on a **comment**.
**Why:** grep is line-based and this phase's subject *is* prose, so every needle is more likely to
appear in a comment than anywhere else.
**Avoid:** **comment-strip before counting, every time.** 17 in-repo `stripComments` implementations
already exist; extract one rather than writing an 18th.
**Warning sign:** the count includes the file that defines the thing being counted.

### Pitfall 3: correcting a citation to a number CONTEXT supplied
**What goes wrong:** §3.6 — correcting "six seam files" to **eight** (CONTEXT's figure) would replace
one false number with another. The truth is **nineteen**.
**Avoid:** derive every integer from source at the moment of the edit. Better: **delete the integer**
and name the predicate (§3.10).

### Pitfall 4: fixing `Retry-After` at one end
**What goes wrong:** the chokepoint fix alone reaches zero wizard surfaces; the client threads alone
reach zero `/process-key` 429s.
**Avoid:** §4.4 — both halves, and state that in the plan. 141 depends on it.

### Pitfall 5: the harness fix breaks unrelated suites
**What goes wrong:** closing the `stubGlobal`/env leak (§8.1) will fail suites that silently depended
on it — 38 files each.
**Avoid:** TRAP-8. Land it **alone**, run the FULL suite (never scoped — CONTEXT §9), measure the
fallout before deciding whether to fix forward or narrow.

### Pitfall 6: opening the PII surface while fixing the CSV breakdown
**What goes wrong:** §12.4 — forwarding `violations` to the browser carries pandera messages containing
cell values, the exact surface QA ISSUE-005 is fenced around.
**Avoid:** if the data half cannot be done safely, fix the **copy** (stop promising a breakdown) and
carry the data half forward explicitly. CONTEXT §7 already permits that.

### Pitfall 7: deleting a "wrong" test that also asserts something right
**What goes wrong:** §9.3 — the match-route 424 tests assert forwarding behaviour that is real.
**Avoid:** re-home, do not delete, unless a producer audit shows the forwarding is covered elsewhere.
