## Summary

I checked these areas and found no defect in them:

- **Atomic window replace.** The DELETE ... RETURNING and the NOT EXISTS check are one statement inside one transaction. A refusal raises P0001 and rolls back the DELETE, the INSERT and the boundary write together.
- **D-02 match.** The worker matches the refusal on the `APIError` class, code `P0001` and the whole message, and never on DETAIL.
- **NULL handling.** The NULL-explicit guards and the 22023 tokens hold.
- **SECURITY DEFINER posture.** search_path and lock_timeout are pinned. REVOKE runs FROM PUBLIC, anon and authenticated, and EXECUTE is granted to service_role only. Both the migration's verify block and arm 9 assert this.
- **Lock key.** The H-1186 key matches `delete_allocator_api_key` and `replace_allocator_equity_snapshots`.
- **FORCE-RLS write.** The write to `allocator_equity_derived` passes on the worker path. `allocator_equity_derived_service_all` has no `TO` clause and gates on `auth.role()`, which a definer body still reads from the JWT GUC. Arm 6 and the live PostgREST probe both cover it.
- **`source` value.** `"mixed"` is in `allocator_equity_snapshots_source_check`.
- **OKX window.** The start is 89 days back, `window_days` is 90, and the start stays inside the 90-day terminus check.
- **Shared-days sum.** Rows are summed only on days every account shares (an intersection), with no zero fill.
- **Retired tokens.** None of them is left in any consumer.
- **Dashboard read.** The boundary read sits at the right position in the `Promise.all` destructure and is non-fatal.

**Can the tests fail?** Yes. Every SQL arm carries a mutation twin aimed at its own line (the DELETE allocator scope, `>=`, the window predicate, the D-02 IF, the boundary IF, the grant, the policy, the guard, COALESCE and the depth CASE). ## Warnings

### WR-01 (MEDIUM, data integrity of the D-03 record): `snapshot_rebuild_boundary` goes stale or loses the real seam

**File:** `supabase/migrations/20261028120000_replace_allocator_equity_snapshots_window.sql:203-218`. The writers that leave it stale are `supabase/migrations/20260602183000_b5b_api_key_delete_atomicity.sql:129-135` and `:238-243`.

**Issue:** D-03 says to "store the boundary date so charts and returns can name it." The record is one row per allocator, and only this function writes or deletes it. Two paths make it wrong.

1. **Stale after a whole-history wipe.** `replace_allocator_equity_snapshots` and the last-key cascade in `delete_allocator_api_key` both DELETE every `allocator_equity_snapshots` row of the allocator. Neither touches `allocator_equity_derived`. The kept older days the seam separated are gone, but the row still names a `boundary_date`. That row reaches `MyAllocationDashboardPayload.snapshotRebuildBoundary` through `src/lib/queries.ts`.
   - One real path is a user who hard-deletes their keys (the cascade wipes the snapshots) and connects a new key. That key's unforced sole-key reconstruct then runs the whole replace.
   - In both cases the boundary describes a jump that is not in the stored series.
2. **A later window call overwrites the real seam.**
   - The function upserts `boundary_date = p_window_start` on every call that keeps an older row. Arm 5 pins this as "a later call moves the boundary".
   - After the first successful rebuild on day X, the jump between pre-rebuild and rebuilt values sits at X-89 (or X-730).
   - Any later forced run on a later UTC day computes a later `window_start`, for example a founder re-run after a D-02 refusal elsewhere, or a retry of the transient marker-failure arm that crosses UTC midnight. The comment at `equity_reconstruction.py:5111` calls that arm idempotent "under the window replace", but that holds only on the same day.
   - The boundary then moves to a date between two rebuilt values, where there may be no jump. The original seam at X-89 is no longer named anywhere.

Nothing renders the field yet (OQ-1), so this is latent today. The field exists so that a later UI-only change can trust it, and with either path above that trust is misplaced.

**Fix:** Clear the boundary record in the whole-history writers, and do not move an existing seam forward over rebuilt days. A minimal shape:
```sql
-- in replace_allocator_equity_snapshots and in delete_allocator_api_key's
-- last-key cascade (same transaction, same advisory lock):
DELETE FROM public.allocator_equity_derived
 WHERE allocator_id = <allocator> AND kind = 'snapshot_rebuild_boundary';

-- in replace_allocator_equity_snapshots_window, keep the EARLIEST seam while
-- older kept rows still exist before it:
ON CONFLICT (allocator_id, kind) DO UPDATE
  SET payload = CASE
        WHEN (allocator_equity_derived.payload->>'boundary_date')::date < p_window_start
         AND EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM public.allocator_equity_snapshots s
                      WHERE s.allocator_id = p_allocator_id
                        AND s.asof < (allocator_equity_derived.payload->>'boundary_date')::date)
        THEN allocator_equity_derived.payload
        ELSE EXCLUDED.payload END,
      computed_at = now();
```
The other option is to store a list of seams. Add a SQL arm for each path: a whole replace clears the row, and a second window call one day later keeps the first seam.

### WR-02 (MEDIUM, operational): transient venue failures become permanent refusals, and MT5 transient arms leave a contradicting `reconstruct_failed` audit

**File:** `analytics-service/services/equity_reconstruction.py:4497-4499`, `:4505-4507`, `:4580-4590`. The MT5 arms involved are at `:4096`, `:4124`, `:4138`, `:4221` and `:4247`.

**Issue:** AR-7 sends every forced job through `_crawl_whole_account`, and its loop turns every non-refusal exception into `ForcedRebuildRefusedError("account_key_read_failed")`. `_forced_refusal_result` records that as FAILED with `error_kind="permanent"`, and PD-I means it is never retried. This includes the job's OWN key.

1. **Previously retryable faults are now final.** A `ccxt.RateLimitExceeded`, a `NetworkError` or an exchange 5xx on any account used to be classified transient: it went through the handler's `except ccxt.RateLimitExceeded` / `classify_exception` arms at `:4777` and `:4818` and was retried. Now any of them is a permanent refusal, and its name says "read failed" rather than what happened. A hang, by contrast, still exits through the outer `asyncio.TimeoutError` arm as transient, so the two failure modes now get opposite dispositions.
2. **MT5 accounts write two contradicting failure audits.**
   - `_mt5_fetch_window` returns a `DispatchResult` only after `_fail(...)` has already emitted `allocator.equity.reconstruct_failed`. That audit carries `error_kind: "transient"` for the timeout, history-unsettled, abandoned-session, client-transient and currency-unreadable arms. It is written under the MT5 key's own id.
   - `_crawl_account_key` then raises `account_key_read_failed`, and `_forced_refusal_result` emits a second `reconstruct_failed` with `error_kind: "permanent"` for the job key.
   - One refused job therefore leaves two failure rows with opposite dispositions on two different keys. The transient one names a key that no job will retry.
   - Census accounts (1) and (2) both hold MT5 keys. 164.6.6.3 D-06/D-16 documents history-unsettled as an expected transient on a fresh terminal login.
   - The operator reading the post-deploy handoff, which matches the `forced rebuild refused (` prefix, gets one verdict from the job and a different one from the audit trail.

This matches plan 07's truth for a rate-limited sibling. The cost was never stated, though: a single 429 or an unsettled MT5 history now needs a manual re-enqueue, and the audit trail contradicts itself. The plan did not decide the MT5 double audit at all.

**Fix:** Keep refusals permanent, but let transient-classified failures stay transient.
- In the gather loop, before converting, re-raise `ccxt.RateLimitExceeded` / `ccxt.NetworkError` (or anything `classify_exception` marks transient) unchanged, so the handler's existing transient arms dispose of them.
- For MT5, pass the `DispatchResult` through when its `error_kind == "transient"` instead of raising `account_key_read_failed`. Either suppress `_fail`'s own audit on the account path (a keyword flag on `_mt5_fetch_window`), or skip the second audit in `_forced_refusal_result` when the MT5 arm has already written one.
- Add a test that a forced account rebuild whose MT5 sibling returns `history_unsettled` ends with `error_kind == "transient"` and exactly one `reconstruct_failed` audit.
