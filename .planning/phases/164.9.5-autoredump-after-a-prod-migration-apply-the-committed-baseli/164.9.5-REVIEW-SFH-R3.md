## MEDIUM

### R3-01 (MEDIUM, CAUSED by the round-2 fix pass: WR-06 placement vs D-32): a stale re-run refuses with a false "truncated" diagnosis instead of the D-31 skip

**Location:** `gateDump`. The `judgeTail` block (and the older `judgeCompleteness` block) runs
BEFORE `judgeMainListing`. The comment on the D-32 block says: "a stale re-run lands on 'ahead'
(skipped, D-31)".

**What goes wrong.** D-32 allows "Re-run failed jobs" on the grounds that a stale re-run lands on
"ahead" and is skipped. A re-run of only `redump-dump` still dumps PROD as it is now. Take a run A
whose `redump-dump` failed transiently, after which a later merge's run B applied a migration
with a `REVOKE`, `DROP POLICY` or `DROP TABLE`. On A's re-run:
- The dump reflects B's migration.
- A's replay set, taken from A's merge, does not carry B's migration.
- So the tail floor throws `tail refused: grants N → N-1 … truncated or narrowed … regenerate by
  hand` before the main-listing verdict would have said "ahead" and skipped.

**Measured.** I called the exported `gateDump` on this worktree at HEAD with:
- attempt 2;
- the committed dump minus one `GRANT` line;
- an injected `mainListing` carrying one extra migration ("ahead").

It threw `tail refused: grants 463 → 462 (short by 1, and no newly carried migration can lower it)`.
The main-listing line never printed. The self-test's `ahead2` arm misses this, because its dump
does not lower any tail class.

**Impact.**
- The red lands on an older commit that was already red, and `main`'s head (B) has its own check
  suite. So no Railway deploy is held.
- The cost is that D-32's promise does not hold for this path.
- The remedy it prints (a hand re-dump from a PROD-linked CLI, the unguarded path) is wrong. The
  right answer was "superseded, skipped".
- The same pre-existing ordering applies to `judgeCompleteness` when a later migration drops an
  extension.

**Recommendation.** Move the `judgeMainListing` verdict (the "behind" refusal and the "ahead"
skip) up, to just after the secret scan, integrity and shape gates and before
`judgeCompleteness` and `judgeTail`. The secret-scan and integrity gates keep running on every
path, as the D-10 comment requires. The two floors then judge only an "equal" dump, which is the
only dump they can judge correctly. Add an arm: attempt 2, main ahead, a dump one grant short,
expecting `changed=false` and the D-31 notice.

### R3-02 (MEDIUM, residual of pre-existing R2-02, narrowed by the fix pass): the tail exemption is all-or-nothing, so a cut in the grant section passes on most runs

**Location:** `judgeTail` and `TAIL_REMOVERS`.

**What goes wrong.** Any single remover statement in the newly carried migrations exempts the
WHOLE shortfall of its class, whatever the size:
- One `REVOKE EXECUTE … FROM anon` exempts losing all 463 grants.
- One `DROP TABLE IF EXISTS` (even in a comment, since the match is not comment-aware) exempts
  tables, policies, triggers and grants together.

Against the committed layout, that gives these outcomes:
- A cut inside the `GRANT` region, lines 14629–16066 (about 9% of the file), lowers only grants.
  It passes on roughly 61% of single-migration runs (170 of 277 migrations carry a grants
  remover).
- A cut between 12029 and 14614 is caught unless the policies class is also exempted (37 of 277).
- A cut after line 16066 (the 12 `ALTER DEFAULT PRIVILEGES` lines) is measured by nothing.

**Why MEDIUM, not HIGH.**
- It needs a dump truncated with a 0 exit.
- The truncated dump reaches only a bot PR, where a human reads a large deletion.
- The bot PR's lane CI would fail grant and RLS gates.
- Nothing merges without a human.

**Recommendation (recorded limit).** Two cheap tightenings, each measurable against the committed
dump:
- Floor the count of `^ALTER DEFAULT PRIVILEGES` lines against the committed dump with no
  exemption, or with an exemption only for a newly carried `ALTER DEFAULT PRIVILEGES`, which 1 of
  277 migrations has. It sits at the very end of the file, so it catches a cut ANYWHERE.
- Bound an exemption by magnitude. For example, exempt a shortfall only up to a multiple of the
  matching statements, and never a drop of more than a set fraction of the class.
