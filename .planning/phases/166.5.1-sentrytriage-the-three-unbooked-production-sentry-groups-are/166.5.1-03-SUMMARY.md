## Re-measured client read timeout

`get_supabase().postgrest.session.timeout`, built with dummy credentials (`SUPABASE_URL=https://example.supabase.co`, `SUPABASE_SERVICE_KEY=x.y.z`): `Timeout(timeout=120)`.

- Installed in the venv: postgrest 2.31.0, supabase 2.31.0, httpx 0.28.1 (`importlib.metadata`; the venv has no `pip`, so `pip show` printed nothing).
- `requirements.txt` pins postgrest 2.33.0 and supabase 2.33.0, which differs from the venv. Rather than rely on the 2.31.0 reading alone, the 2.33.0 wheel was downloaded (no install) and `postgrest/constants.py` read statically: `DEFAULT_POSTGREST_CLIENT_TIMEOUT = 120`. The figure is 120 s on both versions, so the docstring's 120 s stands and there is no difference to report beyond the venv/pin version gap.

**Measured worst case:** an exhausted ReadTimeout costs 3 x 120 s + (1 s + 2 s base) + up to 2 x 0.5 s jitter = at most 364 s, about 6 minutes, per helper call (it was 120 s). Consequences, stated in the docstring and accepted under D-02a / T-166.5.1-07: the claim can keep healthz stale up to about 6 min (90 s `STALE_THRESHOLD`); a `watchdog_loop` iteration can block about 6 min (about 12 min once plan 04 adds its second helper read); a `routers/process_key.py` request can wait about 6 min.
