<objective>
Fix the job-state race behind QUANTALYZE-2A and QUANTALYZE-A at its source (D-03). The watchdog
applies the 10-minute default to `derive_allocator_equity` because the kind has no override. Its
measured runs reach 10:03.9 claim-to-done (PROD, 14 days, 110 jobs, p95 8:14.8), so the watchdog
flips a still-working run back to `pending`, and the run's own mark is then refused (P0002).

Purpose: SC-3. A finished derive result must not be lost or run twice.
Output: one override, a RED-first reproduction of the reset-then-mark sequence, an invariant that
catches the next backfill kind added without an override, and one corrected comment.

**Why the claim-to-done time exceeds the 5-minute handler timeout (read from code, for the comment).**
The claim RPC stamps `claimed_at = now()` on the whole batch (`p_batch_size` 5 in `_claim_priority`).
`dispatch_tick` then runs the claimed jobs one after another (`for job in jobs:`). The watchdog's
staleness clock for a job therefore includes the time it waited behind its batch-mates. On the
dedicated backfill worker those batch-mates are `BACKFILL_KINDS`: `derive_broker_dailies`, with a
15-minute handler, and `derive_allocator_equity` itself. Only an explicit override, sized from the
measured runtime, keeps the default from yanking these jobs. The structural alternative is a lease or
heartbeat that renews `claimed_at`, and the founder deferred it (CONTEXT, Deferred Ideas). Do not
build it.
</objective>
