## Warnings

### WR-01: The 2023-type calendar row now renders at a different scale from its sibling years on wide desktop factsheets (a D-04 change the fixtures cannot see)

**File:** `src/app/factsheet/[id]/v2/HeatmapPanels.tsx:308-312` (new `w`), together with the existing
`YearCalendarCanvas` scale at `HeatmapPanels.tsx:383` (`setScale(cssW / w)`) and the wrapper's
`flex-1` with `minWidth: w` at `HeatmapPanels.tsx:573-574`.

**User-facing:** yes, on desktop. The effect is small but visible.

**Issue:** `scale` is computed per year as `wrapperWidth / w`. Before this change every year had
`w = 770`, so every year row in the stack had the same scale and its week columns lined up. Now a
non-leap year starting on Sunday (2017, 2023, 2034) has `w = 774` on the desktop arm, and every
other year keeps 770. While the wrapper sits at its `minWidth`, this is just the permitted
770 → 774 px growth that the long-label fixture records. When the wrapper is wider than `w`, the
`flex-1` wrapper stretches and the whole year is scaled up. In that case the 2023 row is drawn about
0.5% smaller than the rows above and below it.

**Scenario:** open `/factsheet/[id]/v2` at a 1440 px viewport for any strategy whose history
includes 2023. The article is `max-w-[1440px] lg:px-10`, so it is 1360 px wide. The grid's `1fr`
column is 1360 − 380 − 48 = 932. After the 38 px year label and the 12 px gap, the calendar wrapper
is about 882 px wide. Each year's scale is then:

- 2024 and 2025: 882 / 770 = 1.1455
- 2023: 882 / 774 = 1.1395

As a result, 2023's cells are about 0.08 px smaller than the other years'. Its week grid ends about
4.5 px left of the 2024 grid directly below it, so the week columns visibly drift out of line across
the year stack. The row is also about 0.7 px shorter.

The gate cannot see this. Both desktop fixtures are read at 1280 on the Overview and the composer,
where the wrapper is at its `minWidth` (the fixture records `width 774`, which equals `w`). The
factsheet at 1280 is also at `minWidth` (722 < 774). Plan 07's SUMMARY recorded the effect as "not
measured". UI-SPEC C-5 allows only a change to an already-cut label on desktop. This change instead
rescales every cell of one calendar row relative to its siblings, which goes beyond the dated
amendment's "grow the viewBox right edge".

**Fix:** either option keeps the label whole at 390 px and keeps hit-testing unchanged.

1. **Share one viewBox width across the year stack**, so every row keeps the same scale and the
   columns stay aligned. Compute the right edge in `DailyReturnsHeatmap` as the maximum over all
   years, then pass it down:
   ```tsx
   // DailyReturnsHeatmap
   const isMobile = useBreakpoint() === "mobile";
   const monthFont = isMobile ? 13 : 9;
   const calendarW = useMemo(
     () => Math.max(...years.map(y => yearRightEdge(y, monthFont))), // gridW, grown to the last label
     [years, monthFont],
   );
   {years.map(y => <YearCalendar key={y.year} year={y} w={calendarW} ... />)}
   ```
   A book that contains a 2023-type year then renders every calendar at 774 on desktop, which
   regenerates the long-label 1280 fixture for the 2024 row too. The standard book and the standard
   fixture stay at 770.
2. **Ask the founder about the Rule-7 item that plan 06 recorded.** The 13th "Jan" labels column 53, which holds no
   day of 2023: day-of-year 366 is drawn in 2024's row. If it is dropped for the offset-6 case,
   `w` goes back to `gridW` everywhere and desktop is byte-unchanged. Plan 05's `minPatternTexts: 2`
   and the `heatmap-2023` floor of 16 would then change in the same plan.

Whichever is chosen, add one desktop reading where the wrapper is wider than `w` (for example the
factsheet v2 page at 1440). Without it, this class stays outside the gate.
