<decisions>
## Implementation Decisions

### Area 1 — Scope of the admission

- **Admit `private` ONLY. Do not admit `rejected` or `archived`.** MEASURED at HEAD: the status
  universe is `{draft, pending_review, published, rejected, private, archived}` and the constant
  admits the first three. The phase title says "the owner-only status" (singular); `rejected` and
  `archived` are correctly excluded and must stay excluded — syncing a rejected or archived
  strategy would be a new behaviour nobody asked for.
- **`private` is a TERMINAL status, not a transitional one.** `src/app/api/strategies/finalize-wizard/route.ts`
  finalizes the contribution path to `private` by design. So an omitted `private` is not a
  short-lived window — those strategies stay dark permanently. This strengthens the case that the
  omission is a defect rather than a deliberate lifecycle gate.
- **The constant has exactly ONE declaration and ONE reader** (`ALLOWED_STRATEGY_STATUSES`, read in
  the `strategy_ids` list-comprehension inside the key-hydration loop of `cron_sync`). Cite BY
  SYMBOL — a roadmap citation of `:148`/`:658` had already drifted to `:150`/`:660`, and at HEAD the
  declaration and reader have moved again. Anything that pins a line number here will rot.
- **No migration and no schema change.** The constant is Python. This phase must not touch
  `supabase/migrations/**`.

### Area 2 — The verdict, and it is the decision this phase exists to take

- **VERDICT: `private` MUST be admitted — the omission is a real defect, not a safety property.**
  Evidence: 5 of 5 private strategies sit on keys with no in-set sibling (164.5.1.2's PROD read),
  so nothing syncs them; `private` is terminal, so the gap never closes on its own; and the
  downstream consequence is concrete (Area 3).
- ⛔ **BUT THE WIDENING DOES NOT SHIP IN THIS PHASE.** See Area 4 — it is gated on 164.5.1.4
  SYNCCURSOR landing first. The verdict and the shipping decision are separate, and this phase
  records both.
- **What the omission actually costs, MEASURED — and it is narrower than one prior framing and
  wider than another.** `164.5.1.1-RESEARCH.md` frames the gap as starving the ledger refresh.
  ⚠️ **The brief inherited a correction stating `run_derive_broker_dailies_job` contains "trades ZERO
  times". That literal claim is FALSE at HEAD** — re-measured over the real Python body
  (`analytics-service/services/job_worker.py::run_derive_broker_dailies_job`, 3327 lines), `trades`
  occurs 4 times and `sync_trades` once, **all of them inside comments or log strings, none a table
  read**. The SUBSTANCE of the correction therefore holds: the ledger job runs its own venue crawl
  and does not read the `trades` table. ⛔ Record the substance, not the discredited number.

### Area 3 — Blast radius (traced before any change ships)

- ⭐ **A consequence the brief did not name concretely, and it is the sharpest one.** `cron_sync`
  builds `recompute_strategy_ids` from `per_strategy_stored` entries with `stored > 0`, then
  enqueues one `derive_broker_dailies` job per such strategy via `enqueue_compute_job`. The five
  private keys store nothing, so today they generate **no recompute enqueue at all**. Admitting
  `private` turns on that entire chain: sync -> stored trades -> `derive_broker_dailies` ->
  `compute_analytics_from_csv`. That is new production load and new analytics output for five live
  keys, and it is the concrete mechanism behind the vague "enqueue_compute_job follow-ons" item.
- **This refines, rather than contradicts, the ledger correction.** The daily ledger refresh runs
  regardless of this gap; what the gap suppresses is the **per-sync enqueue**. Both statements are
  true and they are about different triggers. Do not collapse them.
- ⚠️ **A THIRD stranding path already lives in that same block, documented and accepted:** the code
  comment states that a failed `enqueue_compute_job` is NOT re-driven by the next tick, because
  `last_sync_at` has already advanced. Distinct from `SYNC-CURSOR-PER-KEY-STRANDS-STRATEGY-01`
  (that one loses TRADES; this one loses a RECOMPUTE and has a named recovery — the daily cascade
  or a user-triggered recompute). It goes live on these keys at the same moment. Name it in the
  plan; do not silently inherit it.
- **RPC load:** one additional `sync_trades` call per newly admitted strategy per tick.
- **`held` bucket:** the five keys currently land in `held` every tick. Admitting `private` should
  move them out of `held`. The `held` counter and the per-key WARNING volume should DROP — if a
  post-deploy measurement shows otherwise, the widening did not do what it claims.

### Area 4 — Ordering versus Phase 164.5.1.4 SYNCCURSOR (success criterion 4)

- ⭐ **DECISION: 164.5.1.4 SYNCCURSOR LANDS FIRST. The widening ships after it, not in this phase.**
- **Reasoning, and it turns on the asymmetry between the two failure modes.** Today's state is a
  stall that is **LOUD and LOSSLESS**: the cursor holds, nothing is lost, and since 164.5.1.2 the
  condition is *observable* — a terminal `held` status, its own summary counter derived from
  `SYNC_STATUS_SUMMARY_KEYS`, membership in `_NON_OK_STATUSES`, and a WARNING-level per-key line.
  Widening before 164.5.1.4 replaces that with a path that is **SILENT and LOSSY**: on a key backing
  N strategies where one RPC succeeds and another raises, `synced_count > 0` holds, the cursor
  advances, and the failed strategy's window is permanently stranded with nothing recording which
  window was lost. Trading a loud lossless stall for a silent lossy one — on precisely the five keys
  the whole 164.5.1.x line was opened to protect — is a bad trade at any schedule.
- **This is the repo's own standing rule applied, not a new preference:** when something already
  planned removes the risk that would justify a shortcut, the answer is ORDERING, not compromise.
  164.5.1.4 is already in the ROADMAP with `deps_satisfied` and exists precisely to remove this
  risk.
- ⛔ **Therefore this phase must NOT flip the constant.** A verdict-with-a-gate is the honest
  outcome; flipping it now and booking the exposure as someone else's problem is the failure mode
  this decision exists to prevent.
- **The gate is mechanical, not a note:** the plan must leave a gate that FAILS while
  `ALLOWED_STRATEGY_STATUSES` contains `private` and 164.5.1.4's per-strategy resume marker does not
  yet exist, so the ordering cannot be lost to a future editor's memory.

### Area 5 — `SYNC-HELD-CURSOR-REFETCH-COST-01` (success criterion 5)

- **It cannot be CLOSED by this phase, because the widening that would end the stall is deferred.**
  So it must be **RE-SCOPED with a named owner and an accurate description** — never left implying
  this phase closed it.
- **Re-scope to the part this phase CAN honestly close: the unbounded growth.** The held cursor
  pins `since_ms`, so `fetch_all_trades` re-fetches a window that grows monotonically and without
  limit. Bounding that window is independent of the admission decision, carries none of the
  stranding exposure, and makes the stall cheap to hold for as long as the ordering requires.
  ⚠️ Whether to ship that bound in THIS phase or hand it to 164.5.1.4 is a planning call for
  `gsd-plan-phase` to make on cost evidence — but the TODOS entry must end this phase describing
  the real state with a real owner either way.
- ⛔ Nothing here widens a ceiling, relaxes a floor, or adds an exemption.
