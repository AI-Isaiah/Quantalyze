<objective>
Criterion 4: prove the periodic touch does not steal the terminal from live job processing, with a
test that FAILS if it can.

⭐ The naive reading picks the wrong hazard, and the phase's own research disposed of it: D-08's
lease hazard does NOT transfer, verified two independent ways — the terminal epoch binds on FIRST
TOUCH rather than at construction, and the construction fence is a ContextVar that preflight never
carries. The REAL cost is the ASYMMETRY. The heal refuses to queue behind real work by taking a
BOUNDED acquire, but real work DOES queue behind the heal: the three job sites acquire with an
UNBOUNDED wait. So a tick can make a job wait for up to the tick's budget — and that used to happen
once per boot and now happens on a cadence, forever.

⛔ The busy-skip DOCTRINE is not inherited with the behaviour. The boot heal reasons that "a busy
terminal is a terminal somebody is already successfully using, which is itself evidence that the
session is fine and there is nothing here to heal." That is cheap and defensible once per boot and
UNSOUND for a loop: it is a standing claim about session state the tick DID NOT MEASURE, and a
terminal held by a FAILING job is exactly where it is most wrong. The BEHAVIOUR is kept — skip, never
queue — and the CLAIM is not: the tick records `not_measured` (plan 04 gates that half). This plan
records the rationale as `not_measured` reasoning rather than adopting it as evidence.

Purpose: adding a periodic toucher to a shared terminal is the one way this phase could make job
processing worse. The deliverable is the falsifier, not the reassurance.

Output: an interleave test with an embedded negative control, and a positive assertion that the
monitor took no lease of its own.
</objective>
