### The attribution that makes the five spelling rows individual

For each of rows 1–5 the self-test names **exactly one** fixture. The other four spelling fixtures do **not** appear in that row's observer set, which is the self-test's way of saying they still fired: a fixture that stops firing is named twice (`did not fire on its own red fixture X`, then `red fixture X fired {}`), and a fixture that keeps firing is named not at all. One spelling per fixture is what buys that — a single combined five-spelling fixture would have kept matching on the other four alternations after any one was removed, and every row would have read green.

### The one prediction that was wrong, and why it mattered

**Row 4, first run: `missing: ["vitest:shares ONE definition of an app-GUC read with the cron-drift prober"]`.**

The row deletes the trailing dollar-quoted alternation from `DETECT_RE`. My `expect` set said the identity pin would red. It did not — `expect(cronDrift).toContain(DETECT_RE.source)` was still satisfied, because the SHORTENED source is a perfectly good substring of the prober's untouched full regex.

**Containment is not identity.** The pin caught a copy that DIVERGED and was blind to one that SHRANK TO A PREFIX, while its own comment claimed "drifting either copy reds this test". The row still went RED overall (its own fixture and mutant arm caught it), so nothing shipped broken — but a control that cannot fire in one direction is exactly this phase's subject, one layer up. Fixed in `0bfce9c2` by extracting the prober's single-line literal and comparing for **equality**; row 4 is now its standing control in the shrink direction and row 6 in the divergence direction.
