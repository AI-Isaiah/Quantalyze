Each question carries its finding ids (the Phase 155 ledger numbering, `155-FINDINGS.md`), the measured ratio at 3 significant figures, the options, and a ruling line the founder fills in. It holds labels, ids, ratios, ISO dates and day counts only; every amount stays in the gitignored capture directory.

Measured on the sealed 2026-11-19 capture (`155.2-GATE-A.md`), by recomputing each surface's own figure under each candidate convention, singly and in pairs, against the factsheet cell (the persisted Python scalar). The five conventions the plan names (sample versus population sd, calendar versus active days, 252 versus 365, compound versus simple cumulative, rounding) were tried on every finding. A ruling the founder gives here is a decision, never a bucket label this file invents. Where an option says a new bucket, the founder names it and a later plan adds it to `BUCKET_LABELS` with a test.

## G3 conventions

### Question G3-1: first-day return in the curve-derived series (F-155-14, F-155-19, F-155-29)

Findings: the composer returns route's Sharpe against the factsheet cell, one per label: F-155-14 (FX-AI_V), F-155-19 (MM2), F-155-29 (MM3).

The two conventions, each following its own documented contract:

- The factsheet cell is the persisted Python Sharpe. It is computed on the full daily series, the first day included: sample sd (ddof 1), 252 for a traditional strategy, calendar days, so n observations. The stored Sharpe is reproduced exactly from that series on every label.
- The composer returns route reads the stored cumulative curve (`returns_series`) and derives daily returns from it with `equityCurveToDailyReturns` (`src/lib/factsheet/resolve-series.ts`) before `computeScenario` runs on them. That function documents "Day 0 never emits (no stored predecessor)", and its Python twin `curve_to_daily_returns` (`analytics-service/services/wealth_returns.py`) says the same; both are pinned to one shared oracle fixture. So the route sees n minus 1 observations, one fewer than the persisted scalar (242 against 243 on MM2 and MM3, 173 against 174 on FX-AI_V).

Residual, route over factsheet cell, at 3 significant figures: F-155-14 1.01, F-155-19 1.00, F-155-29 1.00. None of the five named conventions closes any of the three. Adding the first-day return to the route's series closes all three: after it the route Sharpe equals the factsheet cell at 3 significant figures on every label (ratio 1.00, 0.999 and 1.00 for F-155-14, F-155-19 and F-155-29). Nothing else moves: the clock (252), the sd basis and the day basis already agree.

Classification: CONVENTION. Both computations follow their documented contracts; the route's derivation never had a predecessor level for day 0. The gap is small (at most 1.01 times) and it is a 3 significant figure inequality, which is the gate the harness applies.

Options:

- (a) Align the surfaces to the persisted scalar's convention: the route includes the first-day return, read from the curve's own first level against its base. This becomes a gap-closure fix in shared code (the curve reader and its Python twin share an oracle fixture, so both move together) with a test that can fail.
- (b) Record a named bucket for this convention: a new bucket label the founder names, which a later plan adds to `BUCKET_LABELS` with a test. The route then keeps its n minus 1 reading by ruling.
- (c) Other (state it).

Founder ruling (2026-11-20): (a), "align the route to include the first-day return. This is a gap-closure fix in shared code, covering both the TS reader and its Python twin, with a test that can fail."

## G4 freshness

### Question G4-1: staleness window between the per-key derive and the strategy analytics compute (F-155-08, F-155-12, F-155-15, F-155-17, F-155-21, F-155-26)

Findings: the six `last_return_date` rows of the Phase 155 ledger. F-155-08 and F-155-12 are FX-AI_V (allocations live baseline, composer book) and are still present after plan 07. F-155-15 and F-155-17 are MM2 and F-155-21 and F-155-26 are MM3 (live baseline, composer book each); they are absent on the sealed capture, and the lags below show why.

Measured on the sealed capture, capture day 2026-11-19, lags in days:

| Label | Per-key returns last day | Strategy series last day | Analytics computed day (UTC) | Per-key end minus series end | Capture day minus computed day |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MM2 | 2026-11-18 | 2026-11-18 | 2026-11-19 | 0 | 0 |
| MM3 | 2026-11-18 | 2026-11-18 | 2026-11-19 | 0 | 0 |
| FX-AI_V | 2026-11-18 | 2026-11-15 | 2026-11-16 | 3 | 3 |

Mechanism. Two pipelines advance independently. `run_derive_broker_dailies_job` (`analytics-service/services/job_worker.py`) says it in its docstring: key-mode "does NOT enqueue compute_analytics_from_csv and does NOT stamp strategy_analytics". So a key's per-key series can run ahead of its strategy's analytics, and the surfaces that read the per-key series (allocations live baseline, composer book) end later than the surfaces that read the persisted analytics (factsheet, route). FX-AI_V is that case: its strategy-linked rows also stop on 2026-11-15 and its analytics were last computed on 2026-11-16, three days behind the per-key series. Phase 155's capture of the same label showed the same shape at one day (series ending 2026-11-12 against 2026-11-11, `155-FINDINGS.md` F-155-08).

What the sealed capture does not say. The docstring explains why the per-key series can be ahead. It does not explain the per-label asymmetry: MM2 and MM3 had their strategy series and analytics advanced on 2026-11-19, FX-AI_V did not. The cause of that asymmetry (no compute enqueued for that strategy since 2026-11-16, or a compute that failed) cannot be read from the capture. It needs that strategy's job history, a PROD read the orchestrator runs, or plan 12's recapture.

Consequence figures. F-155-07, F-155-09 and F-155-11 (G3 section above) are the same three days seen through cumulative return and Sharpe. This ruling disposes of them: they close when the surface series is cut at 2026-11-15, and they stay open exactly as long as the three days stay one-sided.

Options:

- (a) Accept a named staleness window of N days, which the founder sets. This capture measured 3 days (FX-AI_V) and 0 days (MM2, MM3); Phase 155's capture measured 1 day. A later plan adds the bucket with a test.
- (b) Treat it as a pipeline defect: enqueue the analytics recompute after a key-mode derive, as a gap-closure fix with a test that can fail.
- (c) Re-judge after plan 12's recapture, which shows whether the lag recurs on a fresh capture or was a one-off for this strategy.

Founder ruling (2026-11-20): (b), "a pipeline defect. Enqueue the analytics recompute after a key-mode derive, as a gap-closure fix with a test that can fail."

## Data-quality flags

### Question DQ-1: written acceptance of csv_source as explained-and-acceptable (F-155-04, F-155-05, F-155-06)

Findings: the `csv_source` flag stored on all three labelled accounts, FX-AI_V (F-155-04), MM2 (F-155-05) and MM3 (F-155-06). Before plan 155.2-09 it was outside the closed flag table, so each account read as an `unknown` flag finding.

What the code says. Every place that writes `csv_source` is the CSV-to-analytics pipeline or a stamp that feeds or replaces its row. The flag is a provenance marker: it says `strategy_analytics` was produced by that pipeline, whose trade, volume and exposure metrics are NULL by design (the `AnalyticsDataQualityFlags` doc in `src/lib/types.ts`, and the `run_csv_strategy_analytics` docstring). It is in neither guard tuple, so it never promotes `computation_status`, and no reader of the flag does. On the sealed capture all three accounts are `complete`, not warned, with no guard key.

Writer table (symbol, pipeline, verdict):

| Writer | Pipeline | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| `run_csv_strategy_analytics`, success rebuild (`data_quality_flags: DataQualityFlags = {"csv_source": True}`, M-0657) | CSV analytics | provenance, expected |
| `run_csv_strategy_analytics`, insufficient-history, truncated-dataset and malformed-config failure stamps | CSV analytics | provenance on a failed row, expected |
| `run_csv_strategy_analytics`, unrecoverable-failure stamp (read-modify-write, keeps prior guard keys) | CSV analytics | provenance, guard keys preserved |
| `run_derive_broker_dailies_job` strategy-mode prestamp (`_prestamp_flags`) and failure stamp (`_stamp_strategy_analytics_failed`) | broker derive handing off to CSV analytics; a key-mode derive returns before both | provenance, guard keys carried beside it |
| `run_stitch_composite_job` failure and success stamps (`merged_flags`) | composite series handed to CSV analytics | provenance beside `composite` |
| `finalize-wizard/route.ts`, `csv-finalize/route.ts`, `keys/sync/route.ts` failure placeholders | strategy finalize and key-sync entry points, `computation_status: failed` | provenance on a failed row |

What accepting it means, and does not mean. It means an MT5 account's row carries a flag that names the pipeline, and that the harness no longer raises it as a finding. It does not mean the NULL trade, volume and exposure metrics are right for an MT5 account that has a deal ledger: that is a property of the pipeline, documented and unchanged, and is not ruled on here.

Options:

- (a) Accept `csv_source` as explained-and-acceptable. The table change stays as committed in plan 155.2-09, now with your written acceptance.
- (b) Treat it as a defect or as needing another class: name the class and the reason, and a later plan changes the table and its test.
- (c) Other (state it).

Founder ruling (2026-11-20): (a), "accept csv_source as explained-and-acceptable."
