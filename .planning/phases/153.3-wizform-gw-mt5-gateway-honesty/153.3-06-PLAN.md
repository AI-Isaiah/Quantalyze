## ⭐ THE CHOICE: (a) the teardown goes entirely. Stated, with reasoning.

D-35 offers two shapes. **This plan takes (a): the teardown goes from the request AND the worker
path entirely — attach once, never shut down — leaving `restart()` as the ONE deliberate,
lease-held teardown.** The reasoning, in order of weight:

1. **(b) cannot be paid where the teardown actually lives.** `aclose_exchange` is the dispatch
   **epilogue** — it runs in a `finally` AFTER the lock region has already been exited
   (`mt5_concurrency.py:122-125` says so in as many words). Making it re-acquire the lease means a
   teardown that can *queue*, inside a `finally`, on a lock the caller may have just released and
   a concurrent validate may now hold — latency and deadlock risk bolted onto an error path, to
   buy the right to do something destructive. `services/ingestion/mt5.py:246-259` has the same
   shape. **(b) buys permission to keep a call that has no upside.**
2. **The call itself is a measured liability.** Every one of the 9 correlated failures burned a
   *second* full **30.02–30.03 s** in `Mt5Client.close: shutdown() raised`
   (`153-EVIDENCE-mt5-latency.md` §1b). A teardown that does not return, and destroys other
   callers' sessions when it does, has nothing to recommend it.
3. **It buys us nothing.** `shutdown()` "closes the IPC connection. It does not close the
   terminal, does not log the account out, and does not reset terminal state"
   (EVIDENCE §A6 [DOC]). We are not reclaiming a per-request resource; we are dropping a shared
   one. The EVIDENCE's own recommendation is verbatim: *"remove `shutdown()` from the per-request
   path entirely (attach once at boot, never tear down)"*.
4. **(a) is a SINK fix, (b) is a call-site fix.** Deleting the call from `Mt5Client.close()` fixes
   `aclose_exchange`, `ingestion/mt5.py` and both operator scripts in one edit, with **zero**
   per-call-site changes. That is the difference between ending a class and adding a third point.

⚠️ **`Mt5Client.close()` must still release its own socket** (Pitfall 6). "Release my connection"
and "shut down the terminal's IPC" are two different things and this plan is what separates them:
`close()` keeps closing the rpyc transport (plan 153.3-03's `release()` mechanism) and stops
making the remote `shutdown()` call.

⚠️ **The residual we deliberately keep:** with nobody calling `shutdown()`, a genuinely wedged IPC
pipe is healed ONLY by `Mt5Client.restart()` (MT5CONC-01), which is why restart's teardown stays.
All four of its call sites are already lexically inside `async with _mt5_terminal_lock_for(...)`
(verified this session by ast scan), so the surviving teardown is lease-held by construction —
and this plan makes that structural instead of incidental.
