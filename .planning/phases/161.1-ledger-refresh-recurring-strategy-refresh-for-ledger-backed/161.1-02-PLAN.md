**D-09 — the bound, derived not guessed. ⚠️ CORRECTED 2026-08-25: an earlier draft of this
decision costed ONE HOP and named the per-tick LIMIT as the safety bound. Both were wrong. Do not
restore either sentence.**

*The measurement.* `TIMEOUT_PER_KIND` (`job_worker.py:488-504`) puts `derive_broker_dailies` at
900 s and `compute_analytics_from_csv` at 600 s (`:490`), and
`JOB_CHAIN_FOLLOW_ON["derive_broker_dailies"] = ("compute_analytics_from_csv",)` (`:526`) makes the
second an automatic follow-on of the first on the SAME sequentially-dispatching worker (it claims
in batches of 5 but dispatches one at a time — `main_worker.py:606,647,742`). One refreshed
strategy therefore costs **up to 1500 s of worker time, not 900 s**. Costed correctly, the old
criterion "n × cost < 3600 s hourly tick" yields **n ≤ 2**, not 4.

*Why that criterion is the wrong model anyway.* The fan-out only ENQUEUES; the worker drains
asynchronously, so a tick that outruns the drain rate is not by itself a runaway. What actually
stops the queue growing without bound is the pair of re-selection conjuncts: a strategy with a
non-terminal `compute_jobs` row, or with an attempt inside the cooldown, is not selected again.
**The maximum outstanding refresh backlog is therefore bounded by the COHORT SIZE, not by the tick
rate** — 5 strategies today (CONTEXT census) ⇒ at most ~7500 s of queued refresh work in existence
at any instant, against 86 400 s of worker day.

*So: the binding constraint is the 20-hour attempt cooldown; the per-tick LIMIT is a burst /
smoothing cap, not the safety bound.* Write that sentence into the migration comment in those
words. Cooldown **20 hours** on prior ATTEMPT (not prior success): the data granularity is daily, so
one refresh per strategy per day is the correct steady state, and an attempt-based cooldown also
bounds a permanently-failing strategy to ~1 job/day instead of 24. `compute_jobs` terminal-row
retention is 30 days (`20260515113853:198`), comfortably longer than the cooldown, so the cooldown
cannot silently void.

*The two integers.* Per-tick LIMIT **4**, per-venue cap **k = 2**. The cap exists because MT5
serialises on ONE terminal registry (`services/mt5_concurrency.py`) — without the partition an
MT5-heavy book spends every tick on one serialised venue and starves deribit/sfox. ⛔ The LIMIT
must stay STRICTLY GREATER than the cap: at LIMIT = cap = 2, task 2 arm G cannot tell "the
per-venue cap bound this tick" from "the global limit bound this tick", and deleting the cap
leaves the arm GREEN — lowering the LIMIT to the re-derived n ≤ 2 would make this plan's own
anti-vacuity proof vacuous. Worst-case burst is 4 × 1500 s ≈ 100 min of work injected by one tick,
which deliberately exceeds the hourly tick: the next tick re-selects none of those four (in-flight
conjunct), so a cold-start backlog drains over several ticks instead of compounding. Do NOT copy
the reconcile sweep's LIMIT 25 — that sweep's follow-on is pure-DB.
⚠️ Per CONTEXT the live cohort is 5 strategies — these bounds are shape, not volume. Do not
over-engineer them further.

**D-15 — a failed refresh is NON-DESTRUCTIVE. Founder direction, decided; do not re-open.**
`_stamp_strategy_analytics_failed` (`job_worker.py:2582-2604`) upserts `computation_status =
'failed'`, `computation_warned = False`, `data_quality_flags = {csv_source:true}` and — F-4 —
`metrics_json_by_basis = NULL` as an authoritative clear, then calls `_heal_delete_basis_series()`
which DELETEs both `cash_settlement` and `mark_to_market` series rows. Every one of the 5 live
ledger rows is `complete_with_warnings` — healthy, published (CONTEXT census). The MT5 gateway is
on record wedging into `-10005` IPC timeouts three times in one day, and `MT5_ENABLED=false` fails
the whole cohort at once. **So the very first tick after activation, hitting a wedged terminal,
would downgrade four FUNDED MT5 strategies and NULL their by-basis metrics.** A maintenance job may
never un-publish a funded account.

*Mechanism, concretely.* The fan-out already writes `p_metadata` (task 1 step 5); make that the
signal. `run_derive_broker_dailies_job(job)` (`job_worker.py:2368`) receives the whole
`compute_jobs` row, and `_stamp_strategy_analytics_failed` is a nested closure with `job` in scope,
so the marker is readable with zero plumbing — the in-repo idiom is `job.get("metadata") or {}`
(`:7929`, `:8117`). Task 3 adds ONE guard at the top of that closure: when the job carries the
refresh marker AND the strategy's existing `strategy_analytics` row is already in a terminal-SUCCESS
status, record the error WITHOUT downgrading. Otherwise — no prior row, or a prior row that is not
terminal-success — the existing destructive stamp is correct and stays byte-unchanged: an INITIAL
compute that failed has nothing to protect, and suppressing its `failed` stamp would hang the
wizard poller on an infinite spinner (the whole reason the helper exists, `:2577-2581`).
⚠️ Read-modify-write preservation on a live row is not a new idea here: the composite arm's
`_stamp_failed` (`job_worker.py:5433-5482`, M-2) already merges rather than overwrites
`data_quality_flags` for exactly this reason — "a re-derive FAILURE lands on a live row … and keeps
rendering the public factsheet". D-15 extends that reasoning from one column to the publish state.
