<objective>
Criterion 3 — **the heal ACTS on `ipc_fault` instead of reporting `not_healed`.**

Purpose: Phase 164.6.2's heal finally got a production verdict on 2026-09-21 and it was NEGATIVE.
It reported `not_healed:ipc_fault` **five consecutive times** because it cannot drive a terminal
whose IPC is dead — it never reaches a login attempt at all. ⛔ Do not read those five as the heal
being broken in itself: read them as the heal being pointed at the WRONG LAYER for this cause.

⛔ **ACTING MEANS ESCALATING TO THE SUPERVISION REMEDY, NOT ADDING ANOTHER LOGIN ATTEMPT** (D-08).
Re-sending a credential heals nothing when the terminal is not answering — `assert_session_
authorized`'s own docstring says so, and the five readings measured it.

⭐ **WHY THE RECYCLE CAN WORK WHERE A LOGIN CANNOT, and this is the load-bearing insight of the
whole phase.** The IPC-timeout fault means the rpyc BRIDGE IS ATTACHED — its Python side answers —
while the TERMINAL behind it does not. The plan-02 recycle verb acts on the container's process
tree THROUGH that still-alive bridge. A credentialed `initialize()` has to go through the dead
half; a process recycle does not. ⇒ The escalation must be gated on the class where the bridge
answered, and must NOT fire where the bridge itself is detached — there, a recycle has nothing to
talk to and the shipped remedy is a redeploy.

⛔ **`_not_healed` and its prefix are a CONTRACT** downstream consumers key off. This plan changes
what the heal DOES, never what an existing verdict MEANS.

Output: one new outcome kind, one new structured field, one code-gated debounced escalation inside
the existing lease, and the gates that prove each property.
</objective>
