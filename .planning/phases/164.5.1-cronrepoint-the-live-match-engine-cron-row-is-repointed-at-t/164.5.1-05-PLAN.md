<objective>
Close the worker half of the gateway-ceiling inversion. The blast radius reading of 2026-09-13 is
explicit that the 504s are wider than the cron: the same failures hit `main_worker in
_claim_priority` (Sentry `QUANTALYZE-11`, 14 events) and `main_worker in _reset`
(`QUANTALYZE-X`, 3 events), so a fix scoped to `cron_recompute()` alone leaves the larger surface
untouched.

Locked decision recorded and implemented here: CONTEXT.md **Area 2**, final bullet —
*"`_claim_priority` (`main_worker.py:651`) and `_reset` (`main_worker.py:1057`) are IN SCOPE,
consuming the same Area 1 retry seam. Criterion 9 permits deferring them to a NAMED phase; we do
not take that option — they are the same defect through the same seam."* There is no `<deferred>`
entry for them and there must not be one.

Measured at HEAD: both call sites already pass a synchronous closure to `await db_execute(fn)` —
`claim_result = await db_execute(_claim_priority)` in `dispatch_tick` and
`result = await db_execute(_reset)` in `watchdog_tick`. So this is a seam swap, not new plumbing.

⚠️ The subtlety that makes this more than a find-and-replace: the claim site's `except` arm
classifies the exception it catches, and one of its outcomes is a PERMANENT latch
(`_FALLBACK_CLAIM_RPC`). The retry must not change what that arm sees, and a gateway timeout must
never be mistaken for the 42883 that legitimately latches it.

Purpose: one transient 504 currently costs the worker a whole tick and files a Sentry event.
Output: both sites on the retry seam, with the latch semantics proven unchanged.
</objective>
