### What these three readings establish, and what they do not

**Established (measured).** There IS a server-wide `statement_timeout = 120000` from the config
file. `authenticator` — the role PostgREST logs in as — overrides it to `8s` at login. `service_role`
carries no setting of its own at any scope.

**Inferred (stated as inference, not as measurement).** A PostgREST request running as
`service_role` therefore operates under `authenticator`'s 8s, because `SET ROLE` does not re-apply
role settings and `service_role` has none to apply either way. Both possible PostgREST behaviours
converge on 8s. ⛔ The one reading that would settle it beyond inference is a real PostgREST request
as `service_role` returning `current_setting('statement_timeout')`; it was NOT taken, because it
needs the service key and no agent handles credentials in this project. If that reading is ever
taken and returns `120s`, reason (b) for the withdrawal falls — but reason (a), that a per-statement
timeout cannot bound a per-request 504, stands on its own and is sufficient.
