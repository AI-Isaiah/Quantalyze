**The answers to the brief's questions:**

1. **Wedge behaviour.** Yes, `anchor > 1e-7` changes it. For a BTC anchor in (1e-7, 0.001], the
   wedge ratio was skipped before and is evaluated now. It can only add `unrealized_pnl_in_anchor`,
   which means `complete_with_warnings`. It never changes a return, because the wedge is subtracted
   before the roll (`nav_twr.py:871`). See IN-01.
2. **Misleading sub-0.001 returns.** Yes, one can now be emitted (WR-01). I reproduced it: a
   near-total withdrawal leaves a small realized base, and the remaining stop is
   `PNL_DOM_RATIO = 10`. Float noise and pre-inception residue do not produce one:
   - backward-roll noise is around 1e-16, nine decades under the floor;
   - a pre-inception residue is caught by `flow_dominated_guard`, as the new test pins;
   - a zero-NAV or negative NAV still goes to `negative_nav_guard`.
3. **Floor ordering (1e-7 < 1e-6 < 1e-4).** The order is the reverse of USD, but nothing breaks.
   No code compares the three values. The inversion removes a zone that exists in USD and existed in
   the old BTC row: when material < equity < dust, every day is dust-guarded and the job fails
   PERMANENTLY as "material equity but <2 usable days". That is a behaviour change the CHANGELOG
   does not name (IN-02).
4. **Test intent.**
   - The MM-2x Fraction oracle is a strong independent oracle.
   - The [1e-7, 0.001) and below-1e-7 tests bite. Both claims hold: RED on the old floor, and RED
     with the floor at 1e-9.
   - Three test weaknesses are recorded as IN-03, IN-04 and IN-05. The most useful one: no test
     sits at exactly 1e-7.
   - No test pins the WR-01 regime either way.
5. **CHANGELOG, VERSION and package.json.**

## Warnings

### WR-01: A near-total withdrawal now publishes extreme daily returns on a sub-0.001 BTC base

**File:** `analytics-service/services/account_unit.py:57` (the value); guard sites
`analytics-service/services/nav_twr.py:504` (dust) and `:466` (the only remaining stop,
`PNL_DOM_RATIO = 10`)

**Issue:** The intrinsic stop on a tiny realized base is now `|pnl| >= 10 * prev` and nothing
below it. A BTC account that withdraws almost all of its REALIZED balance can therefore publish a
day of up to +/-999%:

- MT5 lets a trader withdraw free margin while a winning position is open.
- The ledger's NAV is realized-basis, so the base after the withdrawal can be a few millionths of
  a BTC while real equity sits in the open position.
- Before D-24, any base under 0.001 BTC was `dust_nav_guard`.

I reproduced it with `combine_mt5_deal_ledger(..., floors=BTC)` at the reviewed SHA:

| Ledger | 1e-7 floor (now) | 0.001 floor (before) |
|---|---|---|
| deposit 0.01, +0.0001, withdraw 0.01009 (base 1e-5 BTC, about $1), close +0.00005 | day 4 = **+5.0 (+500%)**, day 5 = +16.7% | both NaN, `dust_nav_guard` |
| deposit 0.01, +0.0001, withdraw to 3e-7 BTC, a -2e-7 swap/commission day | **-66.7%** | NaN, `dust_nav_guard` |

**Why this matters:**

- A single +500% day ruins every compounded metric on the factsheet: cumulative return, Sharpe,
  volatility and max drawdown.
- The shape is the one the founder described for MM-2x: deposit, trade, withdraw nearly
  everything, then trade small.
- The same economic base in a USD account ($1) is still guarded at $1000. BTC accounts are now
  judged at a base about 1e5 times smaller in dollar terms than USD accounts, and no code or test
  records that this was intended for the post-withdrawal regime.
- The new tests stop at the cases that ARE right: the MM-2x oracle uses a -0.004 loss on the
  withdrawal day and small P&L relative to the base. Nothing pins this regime either way.

D-24's value is a founder decision ("at least to 0.0000001"), and this review does not override
it. What is missing is evidence that the decision covered this regime, plus a test that records
the answer.

**Fix:** Do one of these and record it in CONTEXT:

(a) Ask the founder whether a post-withdrawal realized base of about 1e-5 BTC should publish a
+500% day. If yes, add a test that pins it as accepted behaviour, with the reasoning:
```python
def test_btc_post_withdrawal_tiny_realized_base_is_measured_by_decision() -> None:
    """D-24 accepted: a base of 1e-5 BTC after a near-total withdrawal is measured;
    only PNL_DOM_RATIO (10x) stops it. +5e-5 on 1e-5 publishes +500%."""
    deals = [flow(0.01, 1), trade(0.0001, 2), flow(-0.01009, 3), trade(0.00005, 4)]
    ...
    assert returns.loc["2026-06-04"] == pytest.approx(5.0)
```
(b) If not, keep D-24's 1e-7 for the opening stretch and add a relative guard against the
account's own scale. One option is to NaN-break a day whose prior NAV is under a small fraction
of the account's peak realized NAV, flagged as a new guard key, and never substituted. That needs
its own decision, because it is a new guard rather than a floor value.

## Info

### IN-01: The uPnL-wedge flag now fires on tiny BTC anchors

**File:** `analytics-service/services/nav_twr.py:907`, `analytics-service/services/job_worker.py:5623`
**Issue:** The code's own comment (`nav_twr.py:901-903`) says a near-zero anchor makes
`|uPnL|/anchor` "meaningless" and turns it into a false positive. With `dust_nav = 1e-7`, a BTC
anchor of 2e-7 with any open position (|uPnL| > 1e-8) sets `unrealized_pnl_in_anchor`. The effect
is `complete_with_warnings` only, never a changed return, so this is status noise, not a money
error.
**Fix:** None required. If the noise matters, gate the wedge on `material_equity` instead of
`dust_nav`, which is the "trustworthy anchor" notion the comment describes. That is a separate
decision.

### IN-02: An unnamed behaviour change: (1e-4, 1e-3] BTC accounts no longer fail permanently

**File:** `analytics-service/services/job_worker.py:5582-5604`, `CHANGELOG.md` (0.128.0.1 entry)
**Issue:** Under the old row (material 1e-4 < dust 1e-3), an account whose equity sat in
(1e-4, 1e-3] was dust-guarded on every day. It then hit the material-equity refusal and failed
with `error_kind="permanent"`. The inverted row removes that zone, so those accounts now derive
returns. The change is correct, but the CHANGELOG says only that "nothing asserts an ordering" and
does not mention this effect of the ordering.
**Fix:** Add one line to the CHANGELOG Notes: "BTC accounts with equity between 0.0001 and 0.001
BTC used to fail permanently ('material equity but <2 usable days'); they now derive."

### IN-03: No test sits at exactly the 1e-7 boundary

**File:** `analytics-service/tests/test_mt5_deal_reconstruction.py:845`, `:884`; guard `nav_twr.py:504`
**Issue:** The guard is strict (`prev_nav < dust_floor`). The BTC tests sit at 4e-7 (measured) and
7e-8 (guarded), and I found no test that calls `_guard_denominator` at prev == floor. A `<=` mutant
therefore survives. The brief and SUMMARY describe "the 1e-7 boundary" as tested, but only a
neighbourhood of it is.
**Fix:** `assert _guard_denominator(1e-7, 0.0, 1e-7) is None` and
`assert _guard_denominator(math.nextafter(1e-7, 0), 0.0, 1e-7) == "dust_nav_guard"`.

### IN-04: The pre-inception test's guard-key assertion does not discriminate

**File:** `analytics-service/tests/test_mt5_deal_reconstruction.py:1116-1119`
**Issue:**
- `meta` is account-wide, so any day can satisfy `any(meta.get(k) for k in (negative, dust, flow_dominated))`.
- The docstring says `flow_dominated_guard` decides day 0 once R >= 1e-7, but the test never
  asserts which guard fired.
- The real pin is `math.isnan(day0)`, which is sound. The `any(...)` line adds nothing.

**Fix:** For R >= 1e-7, assert `meta.get("flow_dominated_guard") is True` and nothing about the
other two. Alternatively, drop the line.

### IN-05: A redundant assertion in the floor-literals test

**File:** `analytics-service/tests/test_account_unit.py:130`
**Issue:** `assert btc.dust_nav < 0.001` follows an exact equality to `UnitFloors(dust_nav=1e-7, ...)`
and cannot fail on its own.
**Fix:** Remove it, or keep it as documentation. It is harmless.
