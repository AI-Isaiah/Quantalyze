## The written answer (D-13, criterion 5)

Recorded verbatim here, in M3's header and in the test file's header:

> the CURRENT bridge_outcomes uniqueness invariant is two-part — (i) bridge_outcomes_allocator_match_decision_unique UNIQUE (allocator_id, match_decision_id), one outcome per decision (migration 081); (ii) bridge_outcomes_legacy_per_strategy_holding_when_md_null, a PARTIAL unique on (allocator_id, strategy_id, COALESCE(original_holding_ref, '')) WHERE match_decision_id IS NULL (migration 083), restoring 072's per-strategy guarantee only for rows whose decision was nulled out. Two strategy-sourced outcomes for the same (allocator, strategy) under two DIFFERENT decisions are ALLOWED by design; the old arm's 23505 expectation described migration 072's world.

M3's header explains why 081's own rationale fails without repeating its wording. Migration 074, together with 011's `sent_as_intro` index, keys `match_decisions` uniqueness per decision value. So one (allocator, strategy, holding) can carry several decisions, and therefore several outcomes.

## Deviations from Plan

1. **[Orchestrator instruction over plan text] The lane was NOT rebooted.** The plan says `run.sh down` then `up`, so that the `baseline-replay:` line names M1, M2 and M3. The orchestrator said the stack is shared and must not be torn down, and that instruction wins. M3's DO block was instead proven on the running lane: the control applied rc=0 with its NOTICE, and seven neuters each went RED. The replay set is proven by `check-baseline-currency.mjs --replay-set`, which names all three migrations. What is NOT proven here is the lane's own replay loop reading M3 and writing its ledger row. The lane's next `up` does that. Plan 10's ledger run, or any reboot, will show it. The migration ledger row for M3 is therefore absent on the running lane, which is local-only and has no effect on the tests.
2. **[Rule 2 - correctness] Rows are tracked before assertions**, in (a), (b) and (c). Without that, a regression that wrongly accepted a second INSERT would leak its row past `afterAll`.
3. **[Rule 2 - correctness] (a) reads its two rows back** and checks the two decision ids. Two null errors alone would also pass against an INSERT that silently wrote nothing.
4. **[Rule 2] M3's DO block got a NULL arm and object-existence arms** beyond the presence and absence arms. All of them were calibrated.
5. **Shared-index incident during Task 1's first commit attempt (no lasting effect).** Plans 06 and 08 were committing in the same checkout. My staged M3 was swept into two of their commits (`b91662c08`, `e919f99af`). Those agents then rewrote their commits without it (`810659108`, `bb0fd0b7f`).
