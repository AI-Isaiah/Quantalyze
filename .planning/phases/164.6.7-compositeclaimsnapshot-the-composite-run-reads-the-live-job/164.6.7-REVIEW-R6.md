## R6-01 — HIGH — A programming error in the series heal is retried, and the retry un-publishes the loud stamp that just landed

**Where:** `run_derive_broker_dailies_job` → `_heal_delete_basis_series` (the
`if _programming: raise` arm, SFH-R5-03). The rationale for it is in the
`_READ_PROGRAMMING_ERRORS` block comment ("The heal re-raises although its stamp has also
landed, because that job is failing anyway") and in `_stamp_io`'s docstring.

**What happens.** After the loud stamp lands, the heal raises a `_READ_PROGRAMMING_ERRORS`
member. `dispatch` → `classify_exception` files it `unknown`. While attempts remain,
`mark_compute_job_failed` moves the job to `failed_retry` and then runs
`sync_strategy_analytics_status`. That function's branch (a) fires on
`v_nonterminal_count > 0`, and `failed_retry` counts as non-terminal. For this
job, `v_protect_hold` never holds, because the heal runs only on the unprotected single-key
loud path (`test_the_post_stamp_set_is_the_heal_and_only_on_the_single_key_loud_path`).
Branch (a) then writes, in the latest definition (`20260906120000_computation_error_provenance.sql`):

- `computation_status = 'computing'`
- `computation_error = NULL`, `computation_error_source = NULL`, `computation_error_job_id = NULL`

So the FAILED stamp that just landed, with its curated cause and `writer` provenance, is
overwritten. The row reads "computing" with no error for the whole backoff window. This
contradicts "that job is failing anyway": the attempt is failing, but the user-facing row no
longer says so.

**Where the curated cause survives, if anywhere:**

- `last_error`: no. It holds the fall-through `str(exc)` of the programming error, for
  example `TypeError: …`. The stamp's `scrubbed` is not in it.
- The heal's ERROR line: no. It carries `_read_failure_text(_heal_exc)`, strategy id and job
  id, but not `_cause`. `_heal_delete_basis_series` is defined outside
  `_stamp_strategy_analytics_failed` and never receives the cause.
- Other ERROR lines: only the conditional "no longer protects it" line
  (`_lost_protection_of is not None`). For the common unmarked stamp, no log line carries the
  cause.
- The Sentry capture: no. It is the heal's exception.

**Consequence.** If the retry is deterministic, it re-stamps and the cause comes back.
Branch (b) keeps it at `failed_final` because the job id matches. If the retry takes a
different path, the cause is gone from every durable surface. Examples of a different path:
a transient crawl failure on attempt 2 or 3, or a venue answer that now succeeds partway. The
final row then carries the generic `computation_error_copy('unknown' | 'transient')`.

Before round 5, the heal swallowed the error and the job stayed `permanent`. Branch (b) then
preserved the writer's cause. So this is a regression introduced by the fix round, on a
user-facing surface. It is also the harm round 5 itself cited when it made the tail mirror
log-only ("a derive that ends `failed_final` is one the status bridge reads as a live failure
over the row hop 2 just published").

**Not rare in practice.** SFH-R5-06 says the split is a heuristic: a client library parsing
an unexpected gateway body raises `KeyError` / `TypeError` / `IndexError`. So an
infrastructure hiccup inside `db_execute(_delete_both)` can take this path too.

**Bound:** yes, it is bounded by `max_attempts` (default 3). That means two extra venue
re-crawls and two extra stamp writes per job. The loop is not unbounded. **Acceptable as
written: no.** The problem is not the crawl count. It is the stamp being un-published during
the backoff window and the cause being lost on a non-deterministic retry.

**Hidden by it:** the stamp's curated cause. It ends up on no log line, not in `last_error`,
and not on the row during backoff.

**Recommendation, cheapest first:**
1. Drop the `if _programming: raise` in the heal. Keep the ERROR line and the one capture,
   which already mark "needs a human look", and let the job end `permanent` with the stamp
   intact. This matches the tail mirror's round-5 decision and `_stamp_io`'s own sentence
   "A transient raise there would retry a job whose stamp already landed". An `unknown`
   raise retries the same way.
2. If the raise is kept, pass `_cause` into `_heal_delete_basis_series`. Put it on the heal's
   ERROR line, and re-raise an exception whose text carries both the failure and `scrubbed`,
   so `last_error` keeps the cause. The runbook row must then also name the branch-(a)
   un-publish during backoff.

Either way, correct the `_READ_PROGRAMMING_ERRORS` comment sentence "because that job is
failing anyway". Also correct the heal-programming-error row in
`docs/runbooks/ledger-refresh-go-live.md`, which records `unknown (retried)` and says nothing
about the un-publish.

**Verified negative (no F-5-shaped re-stamp).** The heal's re-raise leaves
`_stamp_strategy_analytics_failed` from inside its caller's `try` body in some places. I
checked every such place with an AST scan of `services/job_worker.py`. It found 32 calls to
`_stamp_strategy_analytics_failed` / `_stamp_failed`, 15 of them lexically inside a `try`
body. The enclosing handlers are only `ValueError`, `CompositeOverlapError`,
`LedgerValuationError`, `NavReconstructionError`, `asyncio.TimeoutError`,
`ccxt.RateLimitExceeded` and the Deribit ledger tuple. None of them catches a
`_READ_PROGRAMMING_ERRORS` member, so the raise reaches `dispatch` unchanged and is not
stamped a second time under another cause.

## R6-02 — LOW — A non-object `data_quality_flags` is dropped at WARNING, with no job id, and then overwritten

**Where:** `run_stitch_composite_job` → `_stamp_failed` (SFH-R5-05, the `_raw_flags` guard).

The fix is correct: the stamp lands once, under its own cause, and F-5 no longer stamps a
second time. That is pinned by `test_a_non_object_flags_value_is_stamped_once_not_restamped_by_f5`.
But the value is not only "dropped". The failed stamp's write replaces the column, so the
malformed value is destroyed. The only trace is a WARNING that names the value's type but not
the job, and under Sentry's `LoggingIntegration` a WARNING is a breadcrumb, not an event.

That is the standard SFH-R5-03 applied one function away ("a WARNING reached no one").
A non-object value in this column means some writer elsewhere is broken, and this line is the
only place that could reveal it. The cause of the failed stamp is not lost, which is why this
is LOW and not higher.

**Recommendation:** log at ERROR, or capture once, and include the job id. A bounded, scrubbed
`repr` of the dropped value would let an operator find the broken writer.

## R6-03 — LOW — The static scan's name list misses I/O helpers defined outside the closure, and the runbook overstates it

**Where:** `tests/test_stamp_io_exhaustive.py` → `_IO_CALL_NAMES` and `_unwrapped_io`. Also the
"CORRECTED 2026-09-26 (round 5, R5 IN-01 / SFH-R5-02)" paragraph in
`docs/runbooks/ledger-refresh-go-live.md`.

The scan cannot pass vacuously. It has a green fixture that must see exactly 4 covered calls,
five red fixtures that must each fail, a `seen_covered` floor of 3 (single-key) or 4
(composite), and an assertion that the named exemption is present. It parses the source
rather than using regexes, so prose cannot trip or satisfy it.

But it matches by name. A direct `await _refresh_marker_live_state(...)`,
`await _refresh_marker_still_on_row(...)` or `await sync_strategy_analytics_status(...)` added
to either closure is invisible to it, because those names are not in `_IO_CALL_NAMES` and they
do not end in `.execute()` inside the closure. Discovery catches such a call only on a
scenario path. The runbook says the scan "fails on any database call outside `_stamp_io`, on
any branch, on or off the thread". That is the same kind of overclaim the paragraph was written
to correct.

**Recommendation:** add the three helper names to `_IO_CALL_NAMES`, with a red fixture for one
of them. Or reword the runbook to "any call through a listed I/O name".

## R6-04 — LOW — The new heal-programming-error row is not pinned by an exact `log.error.call_count`

**Where:** `test_a_programming_error_in_a_post_stamp_call_is_unknown`, and the runbook

## R6-05 — LOW — The same malformed-flags class remains open on the composite success path

**Where:** `run_stitch_composite_job` → `_read_existing_flags` (step 7 PERSIST), which does
`dict(row.get("data_quality_flags") or {})` on the same column. It is not in the diff.

On a non-object value, every successful re-stitch raises at persist, retries, and ends
`failed_final` with no curated stamp. This fails loudly, not silently, and it predates this

**Recommendation:** apply the same `isinstance(..., dict)` guard there, with the R6-02 logging.
Alternatively, record the site as a known sibling in the SFH-R5-05 comment.

## R6-06 — INFO — Tail mirror: DONE on a programming error does not hide the bug

**Where:** `_refresh_marker_live_state(..., raise_programming_errors=False)`, called from
`_refresh_marker_still_on_row` (the single-key tail mirror). Pinned by
`test_at_the_tail_mirror_it_is_logged_and_the_job_is_done`, which asserts that the first ERROR
line carries "programming error" and the failure text, 2 ERROR lines, and 1 capture.

The tail mirror runs on every marked single-key refresh. A deterministic bug there therefore
produces 2 ERROR lines and 1 tagged capture on every such job: recurring and loud, not
permanent silence. One capture per job is enough signal. Hop 2 is already enqueued, so failing
the job would change nothing about hop 2 and would re-crawl. **Verdict: the change is sound.**

One caveat: `_refresh_marker_still_on_row` now has log-only semantics for bugs. Its docstring
says so, and the keyword on the inner helper has no default. A future caller that needs raise
semantics must call `_refresh_marker_live_state` directly.

## R6-08 — INFO — The 500-character test is sound, and the runbook bound is derived correctly

**`test_every_stamp_message_survives_the_500_character_cut`:**
- It builds the real `StampIOUnavailable` through `_stamp_io`, using the real `site` strings
  (`derive_broker_dailies`, `stitch_composite`) and all four `_STAMP_OP_*` constants.
- It uses a 46-character exception class name and a 300-character message, which
  `_read_failure_text` cuts to 120.
- It passes `scrubbed` (message only), which is what the exception's text carries. `detail`
  goes only into `cause`, which stays on the log line.
- Two independent tallies of stamp call sites (AST and regex) must agree.
- An anti-vacuity floor of 187 characters stops it from passing on empty or short messages.

**Runbook bound:**
- The largest row per attempt is a landed loud stamp after a `NO_ROW` breach line (2 events),
  plus a failed heal (ERROR + capture = 2), for 4 events.
- Any other retryable row is at most 3 events, so 3 attempts give at most 12 per job.
- With the provenance refusal line, the worst attempt is 5 events: breach, provenance, landed
  line, heal ERROR, capture. That gives 15 per job.
- A failed re-issue is 4 events and does not raise the ceiling.

Both ceilings depend on the heal re-raise. If R6-01 is fixed by option 1, the ceilings change
and the runbook must be re-derived:
- A heal failure then ends the job `permanent`, so the 4-event row can only be the last attempt.
- The two earlier attempts are at most 3 events each, giving at most 3 + 3 + 4 = **10** per job.
- With a provenance refusal, a failed re-issue is a 4-event transient row, and the last attempt
  can be 5 events, giving at most 4 + 4 + 5 = **13** per job.

The round-4 figure of 9 does not come back, because the heal row is now +2 and not +0.
