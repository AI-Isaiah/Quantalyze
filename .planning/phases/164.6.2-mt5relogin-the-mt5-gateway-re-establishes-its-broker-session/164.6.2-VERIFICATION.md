### The strongest argument AGAINST closing it, and why it does not hold

**The steelman.** Criterion 2 does not sit in isolation. It is criterion 2 *of this phase*, whose stated DELIVERABLE is "make login env-driven … so the session is re-established without a human" — read purposively, *by the thing this phase built*. On wave 5's own evidence the phase's mechanism was inert. The observable was produced by a pre-existing property of the third-party `gmag11/metatrader5_vnc:2.3` image — auto-login from the Wine prefix on the persistent volume — which was true **before this phase wrote a single line of code**. Had the heal never been written, a gateway restart at `10:46:35Z` would have produced the identical 1 → 0 transition. The observation therefore has **zero discriminating power over the phase's own deliverable**: it cannot distinguish a world where this phase shipped from one where it did not. By this repo's own anti-vacuity doctrine — *a test that CANNOT FAIL is worse than none* — that is a vacuous pass. And the founder's entire reason for founder-gating criterion 2 was to refuse "the proven-locally-never-observed shape this milestone removes"; passing it on an observation the phase did not cause arguably re-admits that shape through the other door.

**Why it does not hold — three reasons, in increasing strength.**

**(a) The criterion was written mechanism-agnostic on purpose, by a founder who had the mechanism-specific version in hand and amended it away from equality with the mechanism.** The 2026-09-13 amendment's whole content is the narrowing of the timing claim and the explicit enumeration of acceptable closers. A criterion that lists three permissible non-heal closers is not a criterion secretly demanding the heal. Its demand is *name what closed it* — not *be the thing that closed it* — and the record discharges that demand with four measured facts, three of which are absences I re-derived first-hand.

**(b) The vacuity charge misidentifies the phase's null hypothesis.** The claim the phase set out to falsify is its own goal sentence: *"The MT5 terminal's broker session is established ONCE, BY HAND, over VNC, and **NOTHING re-establishes it**."* That is a claim about the SYSTEM, not about our code, and it was the measured premise of the entire phase. Wave 5 falsified it with a first-party measurement: something does re-establish it, on a gateway restart, in under four minutes, with no human. The observation has enormous discriminating power over the claim the phase actually made — it simply falsifies it in the phase's favour and at the phase's expense. Discovering your premise was wrong is a result, not a vacuous pass, and nothing here is hidden: "THE BOOT HEAL NEVER RAN" is printed in the measurement record's limits section, in the SUMMARY's limits section, and in the ROADMAP criterion the founder wrote afterwards.

**(c) Decisively — the steelman's own remedy is already applied, and applied against the phase's interest.** The correct response to "criterion 2 closed without validating the mechanism" is not to hold a criterion open against text nobody wrote. It is to (i) refuse to let the closure certify the mechanism and (ii) keep the phase open. **Both were done.** `criterion_2_closed: true` and `phase_closes: false` sit in the same frontmatter — a distinction a rubber-stamping record would have collapsed into one field. The three limits are stated in three separate artifacts. A3 is named as untested. And the finding that the real exposure is the dark window, not the recovery, was routed to a NEW named phase with its interval left unchosen because n=1. A record wanting a clean pass had every opportunity to claim the heal worked: the heal was deployed, the session recovered minutes later, and *post hoc ergo propter hoc* was available and cheap. Instead it went and measured four facts to rule its own mechanism OUT. That is the opposite of the failure mode the steelman fears, and it is why this verifier is willing to pass the criterion while refusing to pass the phase.
