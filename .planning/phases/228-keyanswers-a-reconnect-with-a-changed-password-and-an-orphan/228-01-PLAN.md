<objective>
The rotate seam learns to say whether the password actually changed, and its two halves become shared so the connect route can reuse them (Plan 03).

Purpose: CONN-02, founder ruling 2026-11-26 "Validate and update in place" (228-CONTEXT.md `### CONN-02: rotated or mistyped MT5 password`). CONTEXT `### Decided autonomously` locks three things this plan builds: the rotate seam alone validates, one probe and not two; an unchanged password is detected by a constant-time compare in Python and reported as a boolean, and nothing is written; and the seam call plus the owner-scoped UPDATE move into one helper used by both routes, with rotate-secret's behaviour byte-identical (TWIN-8).

Output: `secret_changed` from Python; an optional field in the TS schema; `rotateKeySecret` in analytics-client; the new `src/lib/api/rotate-key-secret.ts` holding `persistRotatedMt5Secret`; rotate-secret refactored onto both; the wiring census moved.

Recorded decisions (planner, under CONTEXT's discretion):
- Helper placement is RESEARCH Option X. The seam call goes into `src/lib/analytics-client.ts`, which is already a seam module, so `rotate-secret/route.ts` stays on the SEAM_IMPORT_EDGE and SC-4f, `EXPECTED_SEAM_FILES` and the five `SEAM_MODULES` copies do not move. Only the wiring census's B-15 site and `EXPECTED_SEAM_CALL_FILES` move.
- Probe-then-compare. `secret_changed` is computed after the success-shape assertion and before `encrypt_credentials`. So the UI-SPEC §1.3 `unchanged` sentence with "and it signs in" is the true variant (UI-SPEC A4).
- The schema field is optional, because Vercel ships before Railway (RESEARCH Pitfall 6).
- rotate-secret ignores `secret_changed` and still writes on every success. Its behaviour is unchanged.
