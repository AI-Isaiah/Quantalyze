### D-54: The factsheet comparator pairs each strategy return with the benchmark move over the SAME interval; the research's "align daily returns by date" is not taken (planner, 2026-09-27; applies Phase 166.4 founder decision D-A to the TypeScript comparator; amends RESEARCH Finding M's recommendation)

It is null, never 0, when strategy date k is after the series' `through`, when no close exists on or before strategy date k-1 within the series, or when a `dropped` date lies after the last close on or before strategy date k-1 and on or before strategy date k. Index 0 keeps today's value (0), so a dense series whose every strategy date has a close is identical to `alignReturns` to 1e-12 inside coverage (the parity pin). A weekday-only fixture pins the Friday-to-Monday pairing. **[Superseded in part 2026-09-30 by D-64 (founder ruling 2026-09-27, 166.4 D-05): index 0 is the benchmark's own return dated the first strategy date and is paired, so the parity pin holds for k of at least 1 and index 0 carries its own literal; the Friday-to-Monday pairing holds for a benchmark with no missing date inside the interval (166.4 review WR-01). ### D-58: A benchmark interval with no point is null, never 0 — for every comparator (orchestrator, plan-check round 1 blocker 2, 2026-09-27; amends D-54)
- **Why.** D-54's helper compounds the benchmark points dated in (k-1, k]. When the benchmark has no close at k (a missing stored BTC day, which 169.2 documents can happen), the interval holds no point, the empty product gives 0, and the next interval receives the bridged move: a forward fill across a gap, which 166.4 founder decision D-A forbids ("skipped, never back/forward-filled across") and which is the fabricated-0 class SC3 removes.
- **Decision.** The helper returns null for interval k when the interval holds no benchmark point or the benchmark has no close dated k; null is excluded from every per-day series and summary, never counted as 0. The same rule applies to the weekday comparators (SPX, GLD, IEF) against a 7-day strategy: a closed-market interval has no point and is null, not a 0% day. A missing-BTC-day fixture that fails on today's code pins it.
- **Reversibility:** reversible.
- **Amendment (planner, 2026-09-27, plan-check round 1 blocker 2): the interval AFTER a gap, stated for the series and for the pairing separately.** The decision above leaves one case open: interval k+1 after a missing close at k holds the bridged point `close(k+1) / close(k-1) - 1` and has a close dated k+1, so neither null condition fires. A weekday comparator's Monday against a 7-day strategy has the same shape (the Friday-to-Monday point sits in the Sunday-to-Monday interval). 166.4 D-A's formula is `price[t_k] / price[t_{k-1}] - 1` with a gap skipped, which needs a close at BOTH endpoints.
  - **Series and summary (per-day returns, and the comparator's own `compute()` windows, cumulative return, CAGR and volatility):** D-58 as written. Interval k+1 carries the move since the last close. Rejected alternative: requiring a close at both endpoints here too. It would null every Saturday, Sunday AND Monday of SPX / GLD / IEF against a 7-day strategy and drop every Friday-to-Monday move from their cumulative return, a new wrong number in place of the fabricated 0.
  - **Pairing (`jointMetrics`: alpha, beta, information ratio, and the correlations, the benchmark-relative metrics D-A governs):** an interval is paired ONLY when the benchmark has a close dated strategy date k-1 AND a close dated strategy date k (D-A exactly). So the interval after a missing BTC close, and a weekday comparator's Monday against a 7-day strategy, are in the series and out of the pairing. The one helper in `align.ts` states both rules and exposes the pairing per index; `comparator-block.ts` and `build-payload.ts` only filter on it. Index 0 keeps D-54's parity convention in both. **[Superseded 2026-09-30 by D-64: index 0 is the benchmark's own return dated the first strategy date in the series AND in the pairing (166.4 D-05, founder ruling 2026-09-27); and for k of at least 1 the pairing also needs every date of the benchmark's own calendar inside the interval to carry a close (166.4 review WR-01, engine fix d1d1fc077).]**
  - **Pins (169-02):** a missing-BTC-day fixture: series[k] null, series[k+1] = close(k+1) / close(k-1) - 1, index k+1 not paired, neither ever 0; a weekday comparator against a 7-day strategy: Saturday and Sunday null, Monday = the Friday-to-Monday move in the series and not paired; the dense-series parity pin and the weekday-strategy Friday-to-Monday pin unchanged (both endpoints have closes). Neuter: the empty product read as 0 goes RED.
  - **Reversibility:** reversible (TypeScript only).

### D-59: A comparator's own statistics annualize on the comparator's own basis; index 0 is null when the comparator has no close at the first strategy date (orchestrator, plan-check round 2, 2026-09-27; completes D-58)
- **Why.** Under D-58 a weekday comparator (SPX, GLD, IEF) against a 7-day strategy is null on closed-market days, so its summary runs over weekday points only. `buildComparatorBlock` annualizes the comparator with the STRATEGY's `periodsPerYear` (365 for crypto); today's weekend zeros dilute the variance so that happens to land near the right answer, and removing them would inflate the comparator's Ann. Volatility, Sharpe and Sortino (and `volMatched`'s scale) by about 1.2x: a new wrong benchmark number. The project rule is crypto annualizes at 365 and traditional assets at 252.
- **Decision.** The comparator's own summary (`ann_vol`, `sharpe`, `sortino`), its rolling vol / Sharpe / Sortino and the `volMatched` scale annualize on the comparator's own basis, `annualizationPeriods` of the comparator's asset class (BTC and ETH 365; SPX, GLD, IEF 252), passed as an INPUT (D-36: switch inputs, add no ratio; the compute-once gate holds). Joint strategy-vs-benchmark metrics (alpha, beta, correlation and the like) describe the strategy and keep the strategy's basis over the paired intervals (166.4 D-A). Pinned red-first by a weekday-comparator-vs-7-day-strategy fixture with literal expected `ann_vol` and `sharpe`, and a neuter that annualizes on the strategy basis and goes RED. Index 0 of a comparator series is null when the comparator has no close at the first strategy date (D-58's "never 0"); where a close exists it keeps D-54's 0, so dense series stay byte-identical. **[Superseded 2026-09-30 by D-64: index 0 is the pricesToDailyReturns point dated the first strategy date (the benchmark's own return for that date), null when there is none; the null half of this sentence stands.]**
- **Amendment (orchestrator, 2026-09-27, resolving the planner's REVISION_CONFLICT in plan-check round 2):** the Decision sentence above covered all four strategy/comparator combinations but its Why analysed only a 7-day strategy. For a weekday strategy with a BTC or ETH comparator the aligned comparator series has one point per strategy date (about 252 a year, Monday carrying the Friday-to-Monday move), so annualizing it at 365 would inflate it by about 1.2x, the same error class. The #597 rule is that volatility, Sharpe and Sortino ride the FREQUENCY clock of the observations. **The comparator basis is therefore the smaller of the strategy's `periodsPerYear` and the comparator's `annualizationPeriods`**, still passed as an input. It equals the text above for every 7-day strategy and leaves every weekday-strategy figure where it is today. A second fixture (a weekday strategy with 7-day BTC, literal values on 252) pins it.
- **Reversibility:** reversible.

### D-64: The comparator follows the engine's pairing rule: day one pairs with the benchmark's own return for that date, a benchmark date missing inside an interval unpairs it, and the base close is the first stored close (planner, revision 2026-09-30; applies the founder ruling of 2026-09-27 "Engine's rule; 169.5 follows" at the foot of this file; supersedes the index-0 sentences of D-54, the D-58 amendment and D-59; amends the D-58 amendment's pairing rule)
- **Why.** The founder ruled on 2026-09-27 that the Python engine's pairing wins (166.4 D-05, ratified), and that 166.4 D-04 and D-07 must agree here too. The engine's rule is `_interval_matched_benchmark` in `analytics-service/services/metrics.py`, including the review fix `d1d1fc077` (WR-01, shipped in v0.108.0.0). The 169.5-01 executor halted at Task 1 at `3357a2eb5` with nothing committed, because the plans pinned the opposite of the engine in two places: index 0 = 0 (the D-54 parity pin, the D-59 index-0 case, neuter F and the snapshot), and an `align.test.ts` case that PAIRED a weekday strategy's Friday-to-Monday interval with BTC's Saturday missing. 166.4 D-04 (both endpoints must be closes, whichever leg is sparser) already matches the D-58 amendment and is unchanged.
- **Decision (1), day one (166.4 D-05).** `series[0]` is the `pricesToDailyReturns(prices, dropped)` point dated exactly the first strategy date: the benchmark's own return for that date, from the stored close before it. It is null when there is no such point (no close dated the first strategy date, no earlier close in the series, or a `dropped` date between them). `paired[0]` is true exactly when `series[0]` is non-null, so the series and the pairing agree at index 0 and the per-day arrays, the comparator's own summary and `jointMetrics` all see the same day one. The strategy's own index 0 was always a real return; the comparator's 0 was the odd one out. Disclosed as the engine discloses it: a weekday comparator against a strategy whose first date is a Monday pairs that Monday with its own Friday-to-Monday return (one M1-shaped pair, 166.4 D-05).
- **Decision (2), a date missing inside an interval (166.4 D-A, review WR-01, engine fix `d1d1fc077`).** For k of at least 1, `paired[k]` also requires the number of `pricesToDailyReturns` points dated in (strategy date k-1, strategy date k] to EQUAL the number of dates in that interval that fall on a weekday the benchmark carries. The benchmark's calendar is the set of weekdays on which the price series handed to the helper carries a close: all seven for BTC, Monday to Friday for the SPX, GLD and IEF fixtures. `pricesToDailyReturns` bridges a missing close into one point and a dropped close into none, so the count catches both, which is the engine's `count == expected`. So a weekday strategy's (Friday, Monday] interval against 7-day BTC needs Saturday, Sunday and Monday closes, and against a Monday-to-Friday benchmark needs the Monday close only. The SERIES keeps its value (the D-58 amendment's series rule): both endpoints are real closes and the bridged move is a real move, so nulling it would drop a true move from the comparator's cumulative return, the wrong number the D-58 amendment rejected. The engine has no per-day comparator series to follow here; this half is this phase's call, recorded so it is a decision and not drift. **[Amended 2026-09-30 by code review WR-02 (= SFH-M-03): the calendar is no longer inferred from the price series handed to the helper. It is a required input fixed per comparator (`COMPARATOR_CALENDARS` in `align.ts`: all seven weekdays for BTC and ETH, Monday to Friday for SPX, GLD and IEF). Why: the route hands the helper a window trimmed to the strategy's range, while the engine infers `traded` from the benchmark's full stored series (`metrics.py`, `b.index.dayofweek`); a short window whose only Saturday is missing dropped Saturday from the inferred calendar and paired a Friday-to-Monday interval the engine leaves unpaired. The coverage caption reads the same masks, so the two agree by construction. The sentence "The benchmark's calendar is the set of weekdays on which the price series handed to the helper carries a close" above is kept as lineage.]**
- **Decision (3), the base close (166.4 D-07). The revision asked for this choice; the engine's rule is taken.** The engine receives benchmark RETURNS, which have lost the first stored price date, so D-07 reconstructs that close as the first return's date minus one day. The TypeScript helper receives PRICES, where the same close is `prices[0].date`, a stored close. So D-07 holds by construction: a close exists at date d exactly when the series handed to the helper carries a non-dropped close dated d, the first one included; a strategy date equal to the first stored close pairs the interval after it (the engine's "a dense strategy older than the BTC window keeps its first in-window pair"); a strategy date before the first close has no close, so its interval is null and unpaired, as in the engine. The plan's existing condition (null when no close exists on or before strategy date k-1) is the same rule stated on prices and stays. No close is ever synthesised on the day before `prices[0]`: that would be a close the TypeScript data never had. The two sides agree whenever the engine's own assumption holds (a contiguous start, 166.4 RESEARCH A1); where it does not, the engine's reconstructed base close has no price behind it and the TypeScript side is the stricter of the two. No locked 169.5 decision forbids the engine's rule. Pinned by one `align.test.ts` case (a dense strategy starting before the first close: index 0 null, first pair at the interval whose left endpoint is the first close) and a neuter.
- **Decision (4), the read's lower bound is now load-bearing.** 169.5-01 reads and trims BTC to [strategy first date minus one day, strategy last date]. **[Amended 2026-09-30 by Amendments A and B below: the bound spans every comparator axis, and the fixture fills only dates before the DB's true first stored date. Text kept as lineage.]** That bound now also supplies day one's prior close, so a trim that starts at the strategy's first date nulls every BTC day one. Pinned by a route case and a neuter in 169.5-01 Task 2.
