## Why the per-run cap selects whole books (revision 1, 2026-09-27)

The first draft capped reconstructs per KEY and refreshed every qualifying key uncapped. That
re-created the item-7 defect: a key beyond the cap, or a sibling of a key that was reconstructed,
got a refresh whose first snapshot row closed the zero-snapshot gate, so its own reconstruct never
fired and its history stayed at zero forever. Two changes close it, and both are pinned by arms:
(1) the reconstruct loop runs first and selects whole books, never splitting one (arms C, S);
(2) the refresh for a zero-snapshot book waits until the book is bootstrapped (arms B, N4).
Consequence, stated and accepted: the cap is soft at book granularity. One book with N qualifying
keys enqueues all N in one run, bounded by that owner's key count. Books beyond the cap drain on
later runs; pg_cron calls the function once a day, and the cap is per call. D-17 carries the
matching "Refined, revision 1" note.
