**The gaps, from VERIFICATION "## Gaps".** G-01: on the /allocations Overview at 390 px, the chart
labelled "End-of-year returns by calendar year, strategy vs BTC" draws two value labels past its
bound on the right (10.73 px and just over 0.5 px); a long percentage label on the right-most bar
is cut. G-02: the 2023 daily-return calendar draws its "Jan" month label 9.41 px past its bound on
the right. The founder's figures are NOT to be written anywhere; this plan reproduces both
defects with synthetic data.

**Why the existing gate missed them (measured from the code, for the executor's orientation).**
G-01: `EndOfYearBarsPanel` draws each value label `textAnchor="start"` at `PAD.left + plotW + 6`
(806 in an 880-unit viewBox), so the label has 74 units of room. The mobile arm draws the
strategy value at font 18 (`stratValFont`), so by `estLabelWidth` anything longer than 6
characters ("+123.4%" is 7) crosses the edge; the seeded book never printed more than 6. G-02:
`dailyReturnsByYear` always builds 54 week columns, and `YearCalendar`'s `monthLabels` loop accepts
day-of-year 366, which in a non-leap year is Jan 1 of the NEXT year, so every non-leap calendar
carries a 13th label "Jan". Only when Jan 1 falls on a Sunday (2023, 2017) does that label land in
column 53, the last one: it starts at x = 14 + 53 * 14 = 756 in a 770-unit viewBox and a 3-letter
label at mobile font 13 is 23.4 wide, so it ends 9.4 past the edge (the founder's 9.41 px at scale
1). At the desktop font 9 it ends 2.2 past the edge, so it is also cut at 640 and 1280. The
seeded calendars are 2025 and 2026, whose 13th "Jan" sits in column 52 and fits.

**Locked decisions that bind the gate.** D-02: "every label's box sits inside its SVG. Labels are
not hidden." D-03 / D-03a / D-03b / D-03c bound which emitters this phase may fix (quote them from
CONTEXT when recording owners). D-04 (as amended): desktop unchanged, proven by a 1280 px reading
before and after. SC-4 pass rule (handoff section 6): zero offenders on the Overview and the
composer at 390 and 640.
