## Summary

All three objects sit exactly where CONTEXT says, and the baseline agrees. The newest definitions are `mark_compute_job_done` in `20260603120000`, `mark_compute_job_failed` in `20260529180000` and `sync_strategy_analytics_status` in `20260906120000`. No later migration redefines, ALTERs or re-comments any of them. The `$$` body of each is byte-identical to the corresponding body in `supabase/schema/baseline.sql`, measured with `diff`. Both mark RPCs already gate the bridge on `IF v_strategy_id IS NOT NULL THEN`. Put the lock as the first statement inside that existing guard, immediately before `PERFORM sync_strategy_analytics_status`. The lock is then the last lock the transaction takes before the bridge. A holder can only ever wait on the `strategy_analytics` row, so the lock cannot join any cycle that does not already exist without it. That covers the 164.9.1 `FOR SHARE` diamond too.

Three measurements on a throwaway pg-lane (PG 16.13) settle the parts of the plan that were open:
1. **`dblink` loads on the pg-lane.** `CREATE EXTENSION dblink` → `1.2`. Two dblink backends give a real two-session test.
2. **D-14's stated RED is wrong, and the gate must not be built from it.** With the lock present, session B waits on an **advisory** lock (`classid=2379582002 objid=… objsubid=2 ExclusiveLock`). With the lock removed, B **still waits**, on a `transactionid ShareLock`: the `strategy_analytics` row A's bridge wrote, *after* B's own bridge reads have already run. So the discriminating observable is **which lock B waits on**, not **whether B waits**.
3. **Both 164.6.7 residues reproduce on the current bridge and are closed by a prototype SQL-only change.** All four existing bridge gates stay green on the prototype, including the SI-02 bounce-recovery Part 4. The negative controls (unmarked job, unmarked sibling, out-of-scope kind, unhealthy row, cww row) are unchanged.

**Primary recommendation:** ship two migrations in one PR:
- `20260926120000`: the lock only. It uses the two-key form `pg_advisory_xact_lock(hashtext('mark_compute_job_bridge'), hashtext(v_strategy_id::text))` inside the existing guard.
- `20260926130000`: the bridge residues:
  - (i) branch (b) clears `computation_warned` on the same provenance-equality predicate that keeps the writer's sentence.
  - (ii) branch (a) keeps a plain `complete` when every in-flight job is a marked, in-scope refresh over a healthy row.

Prove the lock with a **LANE-ONLY dblink gate** whose arms assert the **ungranted advisory lock in the namespace**, same-RPC pairs. Prove the residues with a **plain gate that runs on both lanes**.

```sql
  IF v_strategy_id IS NOT NULL THEN
    PERFORM pg_advisory_xact_lock(hashtext('mark_compute_job_bridge'), hashtext(v_strategy_id::text));
    PERFORM sync_strategy_analytics_status(v_strategy_id);

```
This is the exact line that was applied and measured on the lane in both RPCs.

**Full lock order with the lock added:**
- `mark_compute_job_done`: row X on job J (the UPDATE flip) → row X on each released child C (fan-in UPDATE) → **ADV(ns, S)** → `strategy_analytics` row S (INSERT … ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE; on the INSERT arm, an FK `KEY SHARE` on `strategies` S).
- `mark_compute_job_failed`: row X on J (`FOR UPDATE`) → the UPDATE of J (same row) → **ADV(ns, S)** → `strategy_analytics` row S.
- The bridge's own `compute_jobs` reads are plain SELECTs and take no row lock.

**Can ADV join a cycle?** It cannot, with the lock placed last. The analysis, in five parts:
1. **Who waits on ADV.** A transaction waiting on ADV(S) holds job and child row locks. The transaction holding ADV(S) has finished taking job-row locks. From then on it can wait only on the `strategy_analytics` row S or the `strategies` S key-share.
2. **What would a cycle need.** It would need a third party that holds SA or `strategies` row S and waits on a job or child row held by an ADV waiter.
3. **Every such third party measured on this surface.** None of them can close that loop:
   - The **reaper** (`20260802120000`, bounded by `20260803130000`) is `UPDATE strategy_analytics … FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` on SA rows only. It never touches `compute_jobs` rows and never calls the bridge.
   - The **orphan terminalizer** (`20260826140000`) and **`reset_stalled_compute_jobs`** take `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` on job rows. Neither touches SA or calls the bridge.
   - **Both claim RPCs** take `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` on job rows. `SKIP LOCKED` never waits.
   - **`defer_compute_job`** takes `FOR UPDATE` on its own job row and does no SA write.
   - **`_enqueue_compute_job_internal` (10-param, `20260924230827`)** takes `FOR SHARE … ORDER BY id` on parents and never takes ADV or an SA lock.
4. **Any cycle that remains already exists today.** Any transaction that did lock SA S and then wait on J would already deadlock against a single mark transaction without the advisory lock, because every mark transaction today takes J → SA S in that order. The lock adds no new cycle class. It can lengthen a pre-existing wait chain.
5. **`sync_trades` / `positions_atomic_rebuild`** hold the single-key `hashtext(S)`, a disjoint key space. So they cannot block, or be blocked by, ADV(ns, S). This is also why the two-key form is right: marks are not serialized behind a long trade sync.

**D-12 item 3 (the diamond):** the lock does **not** make it worse. The cycle is made entirely of `compute_jobs` row locks:
- The enqueue holds `FOR SHARE` on B and waits on A.
- `mark_compute_job_done(A)` holds A and waits on B in its fan-in UPDATE.

Neither side needs ADV for that cycle, and the enqueue never takes ADV. With the recommended placement, ADV is taken *after* the fan-in, so it is never held while waiting on a child. The diamond is unchanged and stays a latent 40P01 owned by D-12.

**Why "before the bridge" and not "before the job-row lock":**
1. `strategy_id` is known only once the job row is read. Locking first would need an extra unlocked pre-read, and its correctness would rest on `strategy_id` being immutable.
2. Placed first, ADV would be held across the job flip and the fan-in child locks. The holder would then wait on row locks, which reopens the cycle analysis.
3. D-05 requires the per-RPC diff to be the lock line only.

**⚠️ What the lock does NOT serialize.** Record this in the migration header, and keep the read-order pins, which stay load-bearing:
- The Python **DEFERRED** direct bridge call (`services/job_worker.py`, `sync_strategy_analytics_status(strategy_id)` under `result.outcome == DispatchOutcome.DEFERRED`).
- The claim RPCs (pending/failed_retry → running).
- `reset_stalled_compute_jobs`.
- The orphan terminalizer (running → failed_final).
- Enqueue INSERTs.
- The fan-in release of a child of strategy S, triggered by a parent of another strategy S′ (it takes ADV(S′), not ADV(S)).
- The marker retraction (`_retract_refresh_marker_on_reuse`, a PostgREST UPDATE of `compute_jobs.metadata`).

The lock closes **terminal-mark vs terminal-mark** on one strategy, which is what `161.1-D1` and the ROADMAP goal name.

### Pattern 1: lock-migration self-verify, one statement-shaped anchor per RPC
Anchor on the statement, never on a bare identifier. `pg_get_functiondef` returns comments, so strip them. This single regex pins presence, the two-key form, the namespace, the guard and the placement:

### Pattern 2: the concurrency arm (measured shape)
```sql
DO $$
DECLARE
  cs text := format('host=127.0.0.1 port=%s dbname=%s user=%s', current_setting('port'), current_database(), current_user);
  b_pid int; i int;
  v_ns oid := (hashtext('mark_compute_job_bridge')::bigint & 4294967295)::oid;
BEGIN
  PERFORM dblink_connect('a', cs); PERFORM dblink_connect('b', cs);
  PERFORM dblink_exec('a', 'BEGIN');
  -- dblink_exec refuses a SELECT (2F003); use dblink() and cast void to text
  PERFORM * FROM dblink('a', format('SELECT mark_compute_job_done(%L, %L)::text', j1, t1)) AS x(v text);
  SELECT pid INTO b_pid FROM dblink('b', 'SELECT pg_backend_pid()') AS x(pid int);
  PERFORM dblink_send_query('b', format('SELECT mark_compute_job_done(%L, %L)::text', j2, t2));
  FOR i IN 1..200 LOOP                       -- exits on the FIRST ungranted lock, either kind
    EXIT WHEN EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM pg_locks WHERE pid = b_pid AND NOT granted);
    PERFORM pg_sleep(0.05);
  END LOOP;
  IF NOT EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM pg_locks WHERE pid = b_pid AND NOT granted) THEN
    RAISE EXCEPTION 'TEST FAILED (L1-SETUP): B never waited on anything while A held an uncommitted mark on the same strategy';
  END IF;
  IF NOT EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM pg_locks WHERE pid = b_pid AND NOT granted AND locktype = 'advisory') THEN
    RAISE EXCEPTION 'TEST FAILED (L1): ...';   -- lock removed => B waits on transactionid instead (MEASURED)
  END IF;
  PERFORM dblink_exec('a', 'COMMIT');
  PERFORM * FROM dblink_get_result('b') AS x(v text);
  PERFORM dblink_disconnect('a'); PERFORM dblink_disconnect('b');
END $$;
```
- Do **not** read `pg_stat_activity` inside the loop: it is snapshotted per transaction. `pg_locks` is read live.
- `pg_locks.classid`/`objid` are oids. Compare with the `& 4294967295` form.
- Do **not** add `\set` or other psql meta-commands. The lane already sets `ON_ERROR_STOP`, and `sql-tests` rejects meta-commands.

**Proposed arm set.** Each twin is a PRODUCTION migration edit, layered with its verify anchor:

## Common Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: the oid/int4 mismatch in `pg_locks`
**What goes wrong:** `classid = hashtext(ns)` never matches, so the namespace arm is permanently red or silently mis-keyed. **How to avoid:** compare against `(hashtext(ns)::bigint & 4294967295)::oid`. Measured: `-1915385294` is `2379582002`.

### Pitfall 2: comment text satisfying or breaking verify anchors
**What goes wrong:** `pg_get_functiondef` includes comments. A comment in the body can:
- satisfy a positive anchor over a deleted statement;
- trip a negative anchor (`last_error`, `data_quality_flags`, `sa.computation_warned`, `computing_started_at = now()`);
- shift a count (`OR strategy_analytics.computation_warned` must be 3, the `…_source = NULL` and `…_job_id = NULL` patterns must be 2, the marker list distinct count must be 1).

**How to avoid:** new prose goes in the file header. Strip comments in the new anchors. Carry forward every 20260906 anchor, and add the new ones:
- the unmarked FILTER's `COALESCE(…, FALSE)`;
- `v_refresh_keep`'s three conjuncts with their NULL defaults;
- the new WHEN arm on **both** branch-(a) CASEs;
- branch (b)'s warned CASE on the **identical** predicate as the sentence;
- the new FILTER's kind list spelled identically to `f.kind IN (…)`, pinned with a distinct-count like the marker list.

### Pitfall 3: dblink session hygiene
- `dblink_exec` with a SELECT raises 2F003, measured. Use `dblink()`.
- Consume `dblink_get_result` before disconnecting.
- Seeds must be committed, which is why the file is LANE-ONLY.
- A RAISE aborts the main session, and psql's exit closes the dblink connections, so their open transactions roll back.

### Pitfall 4: expected-red CI checks on the PR
`baseline-content-drift` (already red on `main`), VAC-08 and VAC-04 (until the ack is correct) go red by construction. Branch protection is off, so every gate is advisory at merge. Record the reds in the PR body with their cause. Do not widen anything.

### Pitfall 5: the census pin that gets missed
`lint-sql-gates.test.ts`'s `scanned N file` has been left stale twice. Grep the newest gate basename across `src/__tests__` and move every hit in one commit.
