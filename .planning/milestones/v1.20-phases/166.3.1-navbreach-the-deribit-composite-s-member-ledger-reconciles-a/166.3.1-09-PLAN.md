<objective>
Expand plan 08's tracer to every shape the PROD keys can take, and fence the rule so it can only
make a book better, never worse. Plan 08 proved one path (a Deribit `dust` key with a writer
basis, terminal day equal to its last return day). This plan proves the MT5 paths end to end (the
return-factor roll, no writer basis: a `nonpositive` key emptied by a final withdrawal, and a `dust`
key with a small positive terminal), adds D-08's A3 (a key whose terminal day P falls after its last
return day L ends on L at the writer's own level rolled back through the dropped days, everything
after L trimmed, a Deribit writer basis padded with a zero day on L), which is what makes two of the
three PROD keys compose at all (`166.3.1-SC5-DECISION.md`: plain A read `out_of_window_flow` on both),
and proves the two-candidate book PROD's book 1 is. It then adds the guards at both layers: a refusing or
blocking candidate falls back to today's `DROPPED_KEY` inside the compose, and a compose that still
refuses while a candidate is in play is re-run once with the candidates reverted, so the stored
book row is never deleted because of this rule. Real read failures are never treated as empty, and
nothing after an exit day is booked.

Purpose: SC-5 covers three keys on two venues in two books. Without the two fallbacks, one bad
stored row could turn a book that composes today into a permanently failed job that deletes its
row, which is worse than `dropped_key`.
Output: the MT5 and A3 job-level tracer and shape tests, a compose-level guard file, the A3
roll-back with its trims and pad, the two fallbacks, all allocator suites and mypy green.
