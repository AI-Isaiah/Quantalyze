## Warnings

### WR-04 (MEDIUM, user-facing): an account above a read limit can now never be reconstructed, and the OKX limit is lower than "one page at one millisecond"

**File:** `analytics-service/services/equity_reconstruction.py:838` (OKX stall), `:716` and `:847` (ceilings), `:900` (generic stall). The raise sites feed the unforced job's generic `permanent` arm and the forced `account_key_read_failed` refusal.

**Issue:** a truncation now refuses instead of persisting. That is the right direction for data integrity. But the limits are fixed, so the refusal is deterministic. The unforced first-connect reconstruct ends `FAILED/permanent`, is not retried, and leaves the allocator with no history at all. Before the phase they got a truncated one. Every re-enqueue fails the same way, and no TODOS entry owns these accounts.

The OKX limit is also lower than the docstring says ("a group of same-millisecond fills larger than a page"). Under an inclusive OKX `end`, which is still unmeasured, the `oldest + 1` bound re-asks every fill at `oldest + 1`, and those fills take page capacity.

**Reproduced** with the real-ccxt OKX stub: 30 older fills, 50 at `T`, 60 at `T+1`, one instrument. Under an inclusive end the read raises `BadRequest` ("stopped on a page that did not advance"). Under an exclusive end it reads 140 of 140. So one large order whose fills span two adjacent milliseconds (110 fills) makes the account unrebuildable.

**Concrete case:** an allocator places one market order on a thin OKX SWAP book and fills against 110 resting orders over 2 ms. Their first-connect reconstruct fails permanently, and a forced rebuild refuses `account_key_read_failed` on every attempt.

**Fix (minimal, no new token):**
- **OKX:** on a stall, retry the same pass once with `until = oldest_seen` (no `+1`) before raising. That removes the extra inclusive-end millisecond. The remaining same-ms limit would go away with OKX's `after=<billId>` cursor.
- **Bybit:** when a window hits its ceiling, split it in half instead of raising.
- **Owner:** book a TODOS entry for the accounts the refusal now strands, so they have an owner.

### WR-05 (MEDIUM, data integrity, conditional): WR-03 is only half closed, because `transfers_older_365d` gates nothing and its 0 is ambiguous

**File:** `analytics-service/scripts/allocator_history_probe.py:371-389, 403-415, 732-735`. Plan 06 is unchanged: `git diff b8678f85c..c5452d200 -- .planning` is empty, and `transfers_older_365d` appears in no plan.

**Issue:** the count is recorded correctly. It wraps `fetch_deposits` / `fetch_withdrawals`, which `fetch_ccxt_transfers` looks up by `getattr` at call time, and it dedupes by `(kind, id)`. But the dangerous case from round 1 is "Bybit returns an empty list for windows older than a year". That case prints `transfers_older_365d: 0`, which the docstring itself says cannot be told apart from "the account has none that old". The D-04 runbook reads neither this field nor the new summary totals.

**Scenario:** a Bybit account deposited 400 days ago and Bybit serves deposits for only 365 days. The probe prints 0 and plan 06 proceeds. The forced rebuild then persists a 729-day curve with that deposit missing from the replay.

**Fix:** make plan 06's Bybit rebuild step conditional on the count. If any Bybit account has a 729-day window and `transfers_older_365d == 0`, that is a STOP for that account until it is measured (one read-only deposit query on a known-old account) or the founder decides. Alternatively, clamp the Bybit transfer read to 365 days and mark the replay `pre_terminus`.

### WR-06 (MEDIUM, diagnostic, not user-facing): the probe records a truncated read as `BadRequest`, the same class as a venue rejection

**File:** `analytics-service/scripts/allocator_history_probe.py:603-607` (`classify_failure(exc)`), `analytics-service/services/equity_reconstruction.py:122-124, 682-915`.

**Issue:** the truncation, the two-year guard and a real venue rejection all arrive as plain `ccxt.BadRequest`. `classify_failure` maps all three to `"BadRequest"`, and the probe disables logging, so the WARNING that names the cause never prints. Plan 06 Task 1 step 2 tells the operator to file a failed Bybit member against RESEARCH Open Question 1 (the transfer history). So a Bybit window cut at the ceiling would be misrouted as a transfer-history question, and the probe could not tell "the read is too large" from "the venue said no". On the worker side the gather loop also logs only `exc_class=BadRequest`. There the preceding WARNING tells the causes apart; in the probe nothing does.

**Fix:** raise a phase-owned subclass, for example `class TruncatedTradeRead(ccxt.BadRequest)`, at the nine sites. `classify_exception` still calls it permanent through `isinstance`, so the forced path is unchanged and the token count stays 15. In the probe, map it to an allow-listed `"truncated"` failure class ahead of `classify_failure`.
