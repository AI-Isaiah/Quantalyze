## The calibration — the observed RED, and the isolation finding

Full record in `164.5.1.4-CALIBRATION.md` section 2.

**The neuter:** `and sid not in recompute_enqueue_errors` deleted from the per-strategy advance
condition in `_sync_single_key`, reducing it to the verbatim per-strategy mirror of the key-level
`should_advance_cursor` formula — the shape a reader reaches for by default, and exactly what
RESEARCH's Pitfall 1 predicts a narrower gate would accept.

**Proof it applied:** `cmp` against the byte backup reported `differ: char 39834, line 760`,
`cmp_exit=1`, and `git diff` showed exactly ONE hunk, a four-line condition collapsed to one line,
with no other change in the file.

**OBSERVED RED**, quoted from the run:

> `AssertionError: strat-B STORED but its recompute enqueue raised, so its marker must stay PINNED at the resume point the tick started from — advancing it strands the window whose recompute never fired; got '<a post-tick instant>'`

**⭐ The isolation, which is the finding the orchestrator asked to be recorded either way.** Under
that same neuter, plan 02's two tests **stayed GREEN** (`1 failed, 2 passed`). That is structural,
not lucky: plan 02's injection sets `per_strategy_stored["strat-B"] = 0`, so the FIRST conjunct is
already False and deleting the second cannot change its outcome. Had both gates reddened together
they would have been covering the same thing and one of them would have been redundant — and the
claim "criterion 4 has its own coverage" would have been false while the board looked fine.

**⚠️ A second observation was taken, and it matters.** The RED above landed on

observation was taken with the two row-level assertions temporarily disabled and the same `cron.py`

> `AssertionError: THE CONSEQUENCE: tick 2 must ask the venue for strat-B's un-recomputed window (since_ms=1768435200000), not the advanced key cursor. Got since_ms=1789844390562.`

So the consequence arm bites on this axis too, independently of the row assertions.

## What shipped

### 1. The criterion-4 gate, and why `_run_tick` grew two kwargs

`_run_tick` gains `enqueue_failing_strategy_ids` (defaulted) so the recompute enqueue can be failed
INDEPENDENTLY of storage, and `rpc_calls` (defaulted) so tick 2's re-attempt of the enqueue is
measured rather than inferred. Every pre-existing caller is untouched.

The new test's non-vacuity guards are deliberate and load-bearing: it asserts
`per_strategy_stored == {"strat-A": 1, "strat-B": 1}` and that `recompute_enqueue_errors` names
strat-B and only strat-B, so the test cannot quietly become a storage-failure test and keep claiming
criterion 4.

⭐ **The key-level status on this axis is `ok`, READ off the classifier rather than assumed** —
`strategy_errors` is empty and both stores landed, so nothing downgrades it. From the key's point of
view the tick looks HEALTHY, and the only thing between strat-B and a frozen dashboard is its own
marker being held. That asymmetry is the whole reason this axis needed its own gate.

### 2. The shared helper, and the silent failure it was hiding

`_make_mock_supabase_for_cron_sync` now takes `sc_data` and wires a real dispatch chain for
`strategy_sync_cursors` (`select` → chunked `in_` → `execute`) plus a capturing `upsert` branch
exposed as `mock_supabase.strategy_cursor_upserts`.

⚠️ **The mechanism was MEASURED, and the first draft of the docstring got it wrong.** The draft said
the lookup "raises inside its own fail-open wrapper". It does not. Removing the chain and re-running
the new wiring test showed the mapping came back `{}` with `strategy_cursor_lookup_error` **absent**
from the response: a bare `MagicMock().data` iterates EMPTY (MagicMock auto-configures `__iter__` to
`iter([])`), so `rows()` yields nothing and nothing raises at all. That is strictly worse than a
fail-open raise, because even the fail-open error field would not have flagged it. The prose was
corrected against the measurement before committing.

### 3. The wiring test

`TestCronSyncDeliversStrategyCursorsToFanOut` runs a **real** `cron_sync` with a pass-through spy on
`_sync_key_with_timeout` — recording a deep copy of the key row and then calling through — so the
whole production path runs rather than being stubbed out. It asserts the served strategy is present
with its served value and the unserved strategy is **ABSENT** (membership, not a null), and that
exactly ONE batched marker upsert carrying both strategies is issued through that same real path.

### 4. The held signal, and why it is a fix rather than a deferral

`_sync_single_key` collects `held_strategy_ids`, adds `strategy_cursors_held` to the envelope
**conditionally** (same convention as `recompute_enqueue_errors`), and emits one WARNING per key —
not per strategy — naming the key, the held strategies, and the consequence: the fetch is per-KEY
while the marker is per-STRATEGY, so one persistently-held strategy pins the whole key's window at
its resume point and the range re-fetched grows every tick until the hold clears.

That growth is precedented — the `held` `SyncStatus` comment already documents and accepts exactly
the same growth at key level — and the re-fetch is idempotent by construction, so it is an
efficiency cost and not a correctness or user-facing loss. Under the founder's deferral rule that
makes it fix-or-drop rather than a phase, and it is FIXED the cheap honest way: made visible.

⛔ **No new `SyncStatus` value**, and the code says why where a reviewer will ask.

**Both directions are covered.** `test_a_tick_that_holds_nothing_adds_no_field` asserts the field is
absent when nothing held — with its own non-vacuity guard that both markers really were written, so
"no field" cannot be true for the wrong reason. A conditional field that is always present is not
conditional, and nothing else in this plan would have caught that.
