# Phase 164.5.3 — Security

> Per-phase security contract: threat register, accepted risks, and audit trail.

**What this phase did, in security terms.** Two things, and both matter here: it **widened a
confidentiality boundary** (an existing column, `venue_account_id`, became readable by
`authenticated` and is now rendered on the key card), and it **added a route that decrypts,
re-validates and re-encrypts a live credential**. Everything below is scoped to those two changes.

⛔ **The premise a fresh reader gets wrong.** `164.5.3-CONTEXT.md`'s `# ⛔ AMENDMENT` SUPERSEDES
decisions D-01/D-02 in the body above it. The column was NOT added by this phase — it already
existed (migration `20260812083206`, Phase 154 / WIZCONT-02), was populated by MT5 only, and was
simply never EXPOSED. This phase REUSES and EXPOSES it under **D-01-PRIME**, a founder-approved
override. A threat model written against the un-amended body would be wrong.

## ⭐ T-164.5.3-16 — and a correction I owe the record

⛔ **I rated this `medium` / "human-enforced" when this file was first written, and that was wrong.**
It is `high`, and on the single-PR path the failure was not a risk — it was the **default outcome**.
Recording the correction rather than quietly editing the row, because the under-rating is the
interesting part.

**What I got wrong.** I reasoned that the ordering was documented in the migration header and
therefore merely needed a careful human at ship time. Then I measured what actually happens on a
merge:

- `vercel.json` declares no `ignoreCommand` and no build gate, so the Vercel promotion begins on
  the push to `main` and completes in minutes.
- PROD's migration `apply` carries `environment: Production` — a HUMAN reviewer gate, an approval
  window as long as it takes someone to click it.
- **The frontend wins that race unless a person is standing at the keyboard.**

**And the blast radius is wider than MT5.** `venue_account_id` joins `API_KEY_USER_COLUMNS_ARR`,
the projection behind four live call sites — `queries.ts::getUserApiKeys`,
`::getStrategylessActiveKeys`, `ApiKeyManager::loadKeys` and `AllocatorExchangeManager`'s client
refetch. A projection outside the SEC-005 allowlist answers PostgREST 42501, and `getUserApiKeys`
does **not** degrade on that error — it THROWS, so the page error boundary fires. The allocations
and exchanges pages hard-error for **every allocator, every venue**, for the whole window.

⭐ **The migration's own header had this right before I did.** It names all four call sites, says
`getUserApiKeys` throws, and proposes the split as one of two remedies. The defect was in my
severity call, not in the artifact.

**How it is closed — structurally, not by a human remembering.** Founder decision at ship time:
the phase lands as **two PRs, migration first** (PR #831 carries the GRANT and nothing that reads
it; the projection change and the rest ship second, after the GRANT is applied and confirmed green
on PROD). The race is removed rather than documented.

⭐ **An existing gate confirms the ordering rather than merely tolerating it.**
`src/lib/sec-005-live-probe.test.ts`'s "API_KEY_USER_COLUMNS matches the live GRANT — no drift"
arm projects the roster and fails when a rostered column is NOT granted — roster ⊆ GRANT, one-way.
A GRANT ahead of the roster (PR 1) passes; a roster ahead of the GRANT — the reverse order — is
exactly what that arm catches.

⚠️ **What this does NOT close.** The split removes the race for THIS phase. The general hazard —
a repo where a schema GRANT and its consumer can merge together, with only prose between them and
an outage — is unchanged, and the next GRANT-extension migration will face it again. That is a
real gap and it is named here rather than implied closed.
