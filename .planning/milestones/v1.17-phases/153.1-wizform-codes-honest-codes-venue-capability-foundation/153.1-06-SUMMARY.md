## Task 1 — the third entry, per-route literals, the alias law

**Commit:** `b42cb700`

**(a) The entry.** `finalize-wizard` → `SubmitStep.tsx` / `KNOWN_FINALIZE_CODES`,
`statusRe: "[45]\\d\\d"`. The comment records why it could not have landed
earlier: under the 400-only predicate this route derived **zero** codes, and an
assertion over an empty population is green forever and indistinguishable from a
route with no defects.

**(b) The literal moved onto the entry.** `EXPECTED_SITES_PER_ROUTE = 12` became
`RouteUnderTest.expectedSites`. Both incumbents keep the byte-identical `12`.
The docblock keeps the doctrine verbatim in spirit (hand-typed, never
`derived.length`, and the 12-not-14 lesson) and adds the new one: with a
non-mirror route present, one literal survives only by widening into a range or
being dropped.

**(c) The two 142.2-07 facts scoped, not widened.** `SPLIT_ROUTE_LABELS` drives
the `KEY_INVALID_FORMAT`-once rule and the `EXPECTED_SPLIT_CODES` vocabulary
rule. finalize-wizard is deliberately outside both — it validates wizard
METADATA, not key material, and emits `KEY_INVALID_FORMAT` zero times.

⛔ **Verified in the diff, not asserted in prose:**


No `12`, no member of `EXPECTED_SPLIT_CODES`, and no incumbent `statusRe` is on
a `-` line. The other way to make the third entry fit — widening
`EXPECTED_SPLIT_CODES` by nineteen codes — would have turned a closed-set
assertion into a list of whatever happens to be emitted.

**(d) The mirror pair by label.** `const [a, b] = derived` stays green *by
accident* with a third entry (it destructures the first two) and would compare
the wrong pair the day an entry is inserted above them.

**(e) The alias law.** ⭐ `deriveAliasPairs` reads `SEAM_CODE_TO_WIZARD_CODE`
from source, bounded to its `new Map<…>([ … ])` exactly as `deriveUnionMembers`
is bounded. **Never re-typed.** Both membership assertions now admit a code the

membership second) rather than inventing a second admission rule.

⚠️ The plan's line anchors for the table were stale (`wizardErrors.ts:2157-2192`);

Three SELF-TESTs fence it: the real table is non-empty and carries the

## ⭐ The deviation that is the point of the wave — the code-LESS hole

**Commit:** `d2f9f0a2` · **Rule 2 (missing critical functionality)**

The plan's own `must_haves.truths` says *"The next code added to the route
cannot silently fall to UNKNOWN."* **Tasks 1 and 2 do not deliver that**, and it
is worth being exact about why: an arm that answers with **no code** emits
nothing, so

- `deriveEmittedCodes` never sees it,
- the site count does not move (25 stays 25),
- the union and roster assertions have no code to judge,
- the vacuity floor does not move.

Every guard is GREEN while the route ships a rejection rendering *"We could not
classify this failure"*. That is 153.1-05's recorded open item verbatim:
*"nothing reds if 153.2 adds a twelfth arm without a code."*

⚠️ **And the route-side class sweep does not close it either, though it looks
like it does.** `route.test.ts`'s *"NOT ONE arm answers without a code"*
iterates a **hand-typed `ARMS` table** — so a new arm is caught only once
someone adds it to that table. That is the same "passes by not being listed"
failure the sweep was written to fix, moved one level up. Its positive control
pins `ARMS.length`, which proves the table is non-empty, **not that it is
complete**.

`deriveRejectionSites` derives the population a code-less arm cannot escape:
every `NextResponse.json(` answering 4xx/5xx, coded or not. Segments are bounded
**by construction** (split at the next call) — no lazy run, no character cap, no
way to reach a neighbour's `status:`.
