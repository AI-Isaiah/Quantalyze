## ⭐ The OBSERVED R-7 recovery band — INPUT for plan 140.2-07

`140.2-07` owns correcting the `BREAKER_COOLDOWN_S` docblock and **must take this number from here
rather than re-deriving it**. Observed first-hand, with real timers, against real Redis:

**The docblock at `resilient-fetch.ts:100-107` is FALSE.** It claims 30 s is *"the entire recovery
latency of the system."* It is not. The counter key is never cleared on trip (`recordSeamFailure`
writes only the lock), so when the lock expires the previous bucket is still carrying weight, and
whether ONE failure immediately re-trips depends on **where in the epoch bucket the original trip
landed**.

Both scenarios below waited the **identical 32 s** and reached **opposite** outcomes:

| Scenario | Trip position `p` in bucket | Position at +32 s | Weighted carry-over `floor((1-p)·5)` | One failure then… |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 0.05 | ≈ 0.117 | **4** ⇒ remaining `5-(1+4)=0` | **re-trips instantly** — lock is `"open"` again |
| B | 0.50 | ≈ 0.567 | **2** ⇒ remaining `5-(1+2)=2 > 0` | **does NOT re-trip** — lock stays `null` |

**OBSERVED RECOVERY BAND: `[30 s, 60 s]`, alignment-dependent** — i.e.
`[BREAKER_COOLDOWN_S, BREAKER_COOLDOWN_S + BREAKER_WINDOW]`. The lock's own TTL is a flat 30 s
(observed `[29, 30]`), but the time until the seam actually *stays* usable ranges up to 60 s. This
confirms RESEARCH §3.2's derivation by direct observation rather than by reading the Lua.

## Issues Encountered

**Discovered a real, undocumented production property of the breaker** (this is the "anything real
you found but did not fix" item, and it is the most important thing in this section):

> `@upstash/ratelimit@2.0.8` defaults `ephemeralCache` to a **live in-memory `Map`** when the option
> is omitted (`dist/index.mjs:757-761`), and `src/lib/resilient-fetch.ts` omits it. On a **denied**
> `limit()` the limiter calls `ctx.cache.blockUntil(identifier, reset)` with
> `reset = (currentWindow + 1) * windowSize` (`:1580-1585`). Every later call for that identifier
> then short-circuits **in memory** with `reason: "cacheBlock"` and **never reaches Redis** until
> the bucket ends (`:1560-1569`).

**Production consequence:** once the breaker has tripped on a given Fluid Compute instance, that
instance's subsequent `recordSeamFailure()` calls do **not** touch Redis for the counter at all for
the rest of the bucket — they short-circuit straight to the `redis.set(LOCK, …, { nx: true })`. It
is benign today (the lock write is `nx`, so nothing is ratcheted) but it means the instance's view
and the store's counter diverge, and it is **load-bearing context for two downstream plans**:

- **140.2-06** (per-dependency counter keys) — the cache is keyed by *identifier*, so per-dependency
  identifiers will each get their own independent in-process block. Worth an explicit decision.
- **140.2-07** (encoded expiry in the lock value) — any logic that assumes `recordSeamFailure`
  always performs a Redis round trip is wrong on a post-denial instance.

Not fixed here: out of scope for this plan (a spike + lane), and changing `ephemeralCache` is a
semantic change to the deployed breaker that belongs to a plan that owns the core's behaviour.

**Also noted, outside my control (founder/ops):** the 140.1 review recorded `rulesets: []` on this
repo — i.e. **possibly no branch protection at all**. If that is still true, wiring
`frontend-seam-redis` into the `frontend` aggregator makes it a gate *in the workflow* but nothing
enforces `frontend` as a required check on merge. Recorded, not acted on.
