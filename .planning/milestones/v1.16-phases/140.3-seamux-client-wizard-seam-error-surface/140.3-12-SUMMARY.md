**L1 — `UNKNOWN.cause`**

- now: *"We could not classify this failure, so we cannot tell you what happened or whether your last action took effect."*

**L1b — `GATE_ANALYTICS_FAILED.fix[1]`**
- was: *"…email security@quantalyze.com with your draft ID — we have been notified."*
- now: *"…email security@quantalyze.com with your draft ID and the diagnostics below."*

**L2a — `SYNC_FAILED.cause`**
- was: *"We fetched your trades but the analytics computation did not complete. The failure is on our side, not on your exchange."*
- now: *"The sync did not complete. We cannot tell from here which step failed or how far it got. Your draft is saved."*
- It names **no stage at all**, because `140.3-10` made this the fallback for every kickoff failure
  carrying no recognised code — including a breaker trip in which no trade was ever fetched.

**L2b — `GATE_ANALYTICS_FAILED.cause`**
- was: *"We fetched your trades successfully, but the risk metrics pipeline errored out. The failure is on our side."*
- now: *"The analytics step failed for this draft. We cannot tell from here how much of the sync before it completed. The fault is in our pipeline, not at your exchange."*
- Naming the STAGE is substantiable — the server reported which stage failed. Claiming the earlier
  stage **succeeded** is not.

**L3a — `CSV_SUBMIT_FAILED.title`**
- was: *"Your file validated cleanly, but saving the strategy hit an error. Click Submit strategy again to retry — your data is unchanged."*
- now: *"We could not confirm whether your strategy was saved."*

**L3b — `CSV_SUBMIT_FAILED.cause` + `fix[]`**

If your strategy is listed, the save did complete and submitting again would create a second copy."* / *"If it is not listed, submit again."* / *"If you are unsure, contact security@quantalyze.com with your wizard session id and the diagnostics below."*

**L4 — `CSV_SUBMIT_NO_STRATEGY_ID.fix[0]`**

- **The guarantee is KEPT, because per C-7 it is real here** — this code is CSV-only, and
  `csv-validate` / `csv-finalize` both send the id today. It is **scoped to that path** and the
  internal field name is off the user's screen. It is deliberately NOT widened in anticipation of
  TS-33.

**L5 — `SERVICE_UNAVAILABLE_RETRY.cause`**
- was: *"…so the service can recover. Your key has not been saved and nothing was submitted — this is on our side, not your key."*

**L6 — `SERVICE_UNREACHABLE.cause`**

## Task 2 — TS-17, and 🔴 FINDING-B: the plan's premise for the second throw is FALSE

**The fix.** `data.human_message ?? data.error ?? "Verification failed"` — verified at source: the
seam's rejection envelope is `{ok:false, code, human_message, correlation_id, recoverable}` and
carries **no `error` key**, so `data.error ??` always fell through and a write-capable-key verdict
rendered as the generic string. `data.error` is **kept as the second reader** because this route's own
400/502 arms do emit it (pinned by a case).

### 🔴 T-140-08 is LIVE on this field, and it is not hypothetical

The plan says the check is cheap and to run it. Run at source:

- `process-key-client.ts:568-572` forwards a non-2xx upstream body **VERBATIM** —
  `NextResponse.json(body, { status: res.status })`.
- Python's `_envelope_error` (`routers/process_key.py:375`) applies **no** sanitiser to `msg`.
- `human_message` is **not a closed curated set**: `services/ingestion/okx.py:55` passes
  `result.get("error")` (raw exchange text) and `services/ingestion/csv_adapter.py:123` passes
  `str(exc)`.

⇒ An exception body can reach an **unauthenticated browser**. So the field is surfaced behind
`safeHumanMessage`, a **SHAPE denylist** (URL, any scheme, hostname, IP, bare 3-digit status,
traceback markers, 200-char bound) — following `140.3-11`'s `seamDependencyName` discipline.

⚠️ **Deliberately a denylist, never an allow-list of known sentences.** An allow-list's failure mode
is that a real, correct rejection renders as the generic *"Verification failed"* — which is the exact
defect TS-17 exists to fix. The **positive** case (an ordinary curated verdict passes through
untouched) is the load-bearing one: without it, a guard that rejected everything would satisfy every
disclosure assertion while silently re-creating the defect.

## 🔴 FINDING-C — closing `140.3-10`'s two 400 arms FORCED a TRAP-4 guard

### The behaviour change, declared

Wiring the arms **created** a fresh TRAP-4 at the site `140.3-10` had just guarded:
`VALIDATION_FAILED` is non-recoverable (`actions: ["request_call"]`), so no Retry renders, and the
**sole** surviving control in that render was *"Try another key"* — which fires
`handleDeleteDraft()`. That is telling a user *"our software sent a request we could not read"* and
offering them exactly one button, which **destroys their draft**. Swapping the user's key cannot fix
a bug in our own page.

**Change:** the destructive-control check widened from an `===` on one code to a **hand-typed set**,
`errorStateIsDraftGone` → `errorStateHidesDestructiveControl`.

- It is **additive**: `GATE_DRAFT_GONE`'s behaviour is byte-identical, and `140.3-10`'s
  anti-regression case (*"every OTHER error state keeps 'Try another key'"*) drives an **uncoded
  500**, so it does not redden — verified, not assumed.
- **No `actions` array was edited**, so no code's `recoverable` moved and `140.3-10`'s table-wide
  TRAP-4 guard is untouched.
- **M66c applies unchanged under the new name** — recorded in both ledgers so the row stays runnable.

Both 400 arms are asserted **separately**, because two wire codes on one route is exactly the shape
where one of a pair gets wired and the class is reported closed.
