# Phase 141.2 Plan 02: resync's retry verdict follows its own evidence — Summary

Withdrew `resync`'s seam-retry grant (finding 6 / D-03) by moving it from
`RETRY_SAFE_FLOW_TYPES` to `RETRY_AUDIT_NO_FLOW_TYPES`, rewrote the four pins the
move breaks in the same commit, replaced a count fence that was structurally
incapable of seeing the move, and corrected the Python-side comment that had been
making the same over-claim since Phase 141.

---

## ⚠️ FOUNDER-VISIBLE SCOPE CHANGE — stated plainly, not buried

**The `/process-key` seam retry is now enabled for `onboard` alone.**

`resync` was **20 of 42 all-time `/process-key` audit rows — 48%**, and 10× the
onboard volume (2 rows). Withdrawing it removes essentially all of the retry
traffic the 141 rollout actually served. Combined with plan 05 (D-01), which makes
`onboard`'s grant conditional on a usable idempotency key being present, the honest
description of the shipped retry surface is **`onboard` WITH a session id, and
nothing else**.

This is not a tightening of a marginal edge case. **141's retry rollout was broader
than its evidence supported**, on both of the two flows it enabled:

| Flow | 141 shipped | After 141.2 | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| `resync` | retries once | **NO verdict** | the pre-check does not make a replay safe; the worker tick can advance the draft between attempts |
| `onboard` | retries once | conditional (plan 05) | the idempotency key the evidence assumes is not always sent |
| `teaser`, `csv` | no retry | unchanged | — |

**What is NOT claimed:** this does not mean duplicate rows were being written in
production at volume. It means the *written justification* for retrying resync was
false, and a retry was being issued on it. The residual it exposes is bounded — one
extra draft `strategy_verifications` row, self-healing on the next resync, with the
compute job still deduped by `compute_jobs_one_inflight_per_kind_strategy`.

**Consequence for D-07 (finding 8, limiter double-spend):** the re-measure input is
now settled — the retry-eligible population falls to `onboard`-with-a-key, which is
2 of 42 historical requests. Plan 06 records the disposition.

## The defect, precisely

141.1-02 re-derived `resync`'s retry-safety evidence, found the sentence the grant
rested on — *"The SEQUENTIAL-retry class is closed"* — to be false, and **deleted
it**. It did not withdraw the `retries: 1` that sentence had justified. The verdict
outlived its own evidence.

The window is real, not theoretical: the compute worker's 30 s tick advances SV#1
out of `status='draft'` during the 250–500 ms backoff, so attempt 2's
strategy-scoped pre-check matches nothing and inserts SV#2.

**A MAP MOVE, not a field edit.** `RetrySafeEntry.retries` is the literal type `1`
by deliberate design ("widening to N>1 is a deliberate type change, not a data
edit"), so `resync` could not simply carry `retries: 0`.

### The count fence reddens on a map move in EITHER direction — confirmed

This was the plan's trap 3, and it is worth stating as a measured result rather
than a claim:

| Direction | per-map fences | total-only fence (`.toBe(13)`) |
|---|---|---|
| resync YES → NO (the real fix) | **RED** (`expected 2 to be 1`, `expected 2 to be 3`) | **GREEN** — 2+4+2+5 = 13 |
| resync NO → YES (SC-D revert) | **RED** (`expected 1 to be 2`, `expected 3 to be 2`) | **GREEN** — 1+4+3+5 = 13 |

Both populations sum to 13. The pre-existing
`EXPECTED_EVIDENCE_STRING_COUNT = 2 + 4 + 2 + 5` would have **stayed green in both
directions while its own failure message ("2 flow YES … 2 flow NO") became false** —
a fence reporting agreement about numbers it was no longer checking. Replaced with
four per-map hand-typed literals, each asserted against its own map, with the total
*derived* from them (kept only to catch a fifth map not wired into the flattener).
