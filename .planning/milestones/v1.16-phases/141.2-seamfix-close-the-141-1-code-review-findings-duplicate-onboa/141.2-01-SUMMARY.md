# Phase 141.2 Plan 01: the breaker cluster (findings 10, 11, 5) Summary

**The seam breaker can arm again — the trip path branches on the raw store value instead of a decode that conflated "absent" with "corrupt", `decodeBreakerLock` rejects implausible absolute epochs one-sidedly, and each retry attempt is now judged against its own admission instant.**

## Performance

- **Duration:** ~35 min of execution across two sessions (Task 1: 2026-08-01 ~11:34 +02:00; Task 2 after a host power-off: 2026-08-01 17:38–17:42 +02:00)
- **Tasks:** 2 of 2
- **Files modified:** 6 (2 production, 2 test, 2 docs/planning)

## Accomplishments

- **Finding 10 (the phase's top priority, a live 141.1 regression attributable to `f308b460`) closed.** `recordSeamFailure`'s trip path captures `rawLock` and decides its write from that value's presence. `decodeBreakerLock` answers `null` for two different store states — key ABSENT and key PRESENT-but-undecodable — and only the first justifies `SET NX`. A corrupt-but-present value therefore took the `nx` arm, Redis refused it because the key exists, no lock was written and no transition was emitted: the circuit could not open for that key's entire TTL, across all fifteen seam routes, silently. The absent-key `nx` arm and the displacement arm's ownership rule are unchanged, and there are zero extra store round trips (the SC-4b headroom ceiling requires that).
- **Finding 11 closed.** `decodeBreakerLock` gained an optional `nowMs` parameter and a one-sided absolute plausibility bound: `expiresAtMs > nowMs + MAX_BREAKER_LOCK_SPAN_MS ⇒ null`. `open:100000000000000000:100000000000030000` has a legal 30 000 ms span, used to decode cleanly, and minted a `Retry-After` of ~1e14 that A-15 accepted as an integer and both seam clients put on the wire — including to the anonymous teaser. The past side stays unbounded on purpose.
- **Finding 5 / D-09 closed.** `admittedAtMs` is a `let`, reassigned immediately after each successful pre-attempt breaker re-check. A-25's predicate is "was this request already IN FLIGHT when that lock was armed", and "in flight" is a property of an ATTEMPT: attempt 2 is admitted separately, past its own re-check of a by-then-expired lock, so its failure is evidence gathered entirely after the arming. Captured once above the loop, that timestamp made every retried wave's evidence look stale — on the 30 s `optimize-weights` budget the breaker could never re-arm from a retried call, so allocators waited 60 s+ per click instead of receiving an immediate `CIRCUIT_OPEN` 503.
- **All three ledger mutations (SC-A, SC-B, SC-C) observed RED with real output, then restored GREEN**, with the tombstone guard green throughout both decoder mutations.

## Decisions Made

- **The recapture goes after the `CircuitOpenError` throw, not before the re-check.** An attempt the breaker refuses is never admitted; stamping it as admitted would hand A-25 an admission instant no request ever had.
- **The mid-flight lock's span in the D-09 pins is 10 000 ms, not production's 30 000 ms.** The fake limiter's counting window is 30 000 ms, so a timeline advancing the frozen clock past it would roll the failure counter over and the trip path — the only path A-25 lives on — would stop being reached. Nothing in the cases turns on the span's magnitude; they turn on the ORDER of admission, arming and expiry. Documented at the constant.
- **`Date.now` is frozen and hand-advanced rather than left to wall time.** It is the single time source read by the admission timestamp, the A-25 comparison, the lock's encoded expiry and the fake store's TTL eviction. Real timers stay real, so the backoff still awaits a genuine `setTimeout`.
- **The test-side lock decoder is hand-typed, not `decodeBreakerLock`.** Same oracle-independence rule the file's backoff literals follow: an assertion that parses with production's parser cannot contradict production's parser. The harness-side ENCODER is shared deliberately — it is pinned literal-against-literal in `seam-constants.pin.test.ts`.
